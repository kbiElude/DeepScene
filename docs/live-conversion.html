Live conversion

The converter lives inside the program it converts. It watches each call, decides how a draw is treated, renders the second eye, and hands both images to another process. This page covers its structure, the resolution, the pacing, the adapters of Direct3D 7 and OpenGL, the frame channel and the log.

Inside the program

Decisions and adapters

The converter is made of an adapter, which knows the API of the application, and of decisions that do not. StereoCore, ConvergenceModel, MatrixSlots, the frame channel, the presenter and the VR player have no API in them: an adapter tells StereoCore what the application does (it sets a matrix, it draws) and does what it answers.

The pieces

PieceJob
ConverterRegisters the callbacks and orders the work of each call: feed the analysis, keep matrices, classify and render draws, take the settings the VR player changed.
CallFeederGives the analysis each call as a dump would, for Direct3D 7, Direct3D 8 and OpenGL alike; keeps the calls that wait for results in per-thread pools, which hand their vectors back to the next call.
StereoCoreThe decisions: the class of a draw from the recipe and the view matrix, unknown draws and frames. Owns ConvergenceModel (where the eyes converge and how far apart they are) and MatrixSlots (the matrices a rule is about, and which to put in before a draw).
The adapter for an APIRenders the second eye, scales the resolution, shows the program in a window or hides it, and exports the frames. The classes are listed below.
FramePasser, SessionLogWhat the adapters share: pass the presented frame on to the channel (at most one in 8 ms) and wait for the headset to want the next (HostMemoryExporter, the base of the three exporters, does the waiting); one log line per event.

Details of the converter and the feeder

  • Converter keeps what it knows of the running program (the core, the feeder, the renderers, the counters) in one struct behind one lock (Utils::Parallel::SrwMutex), and no other lock is taken while it is held.
  • Callback times are sampled, one call in 61.
  • CallFeeder takes the data chunks of a call out of the interceptor in one go, into vectors from a pool (APIInterceptor::ChunkPool) which the next call gets back, so that a chunk is not an allocation.
  • The calls it makes carry no timestamp (timestamp_qpc is 0): nothing in the converter looks at when a call was made.

What a converter cannot know

What a converter can know when a draw is issued is less than a replay knows: the pass is not known at that point, so a draw is told by its signature and the view matrix, and a rule about a pass or a frame shape cannot be followed. Where a program needs more than that, the converter fails at once through StereoCore::require_fast_path instead of producing wrong images.

  • The same goes for what an adapter cannot follow: a program that locks a render target, or resets the device.
  • The Direct3D 8 adapter ends the process, with a message that says so, when the program draws vertices positioned in pixels (D3DFVF_XYZRHW, as a HUD or text uses) from a vertex buffer.
  • The ones that the call carries (DrawPrimitiveUP, DrawIndexedPrimitiveUP) are drawn for both eyes by the renderer (see Vertices in pixels).

A frame

  1. The program sets matrices and draws. The hook gives each call to the feeder. A draw is analysed immediately (its signature is needed before it runs); other calls are kept until their results are in.
  2. The core looks the draw up in the recipe. A scene draw is made for each eye with the eye's matrices; an interface draw is made once; a skipped one is not made. The maths of the matrices, the separation and the convergence is on the Stereo page.
  3. At the end of the frame the images go to the frame channel.

How the images reach the channel (Direct3D 8)

  • The GPU copies the images to system-memory surfaces that the exporter keeps. There are two sets of surfaces.
  • A thread of the exporter (a Utils::Parallel::JobThread) copies the rows to the channel while the program renders the next frame. The rows of the right eye go through a second thread.
  • The program waits for the thread only when it has the next frame to hand over.
  • The exporters of Direct3D 7 and OpenGL do their own version of that.

Resolution

A program that asks for a back buffer of 800 × 600 gives a small picture in a headset. The converter therefore renders it larger, at the resolution of the headset.

Where the size comes from

  1. The presenter asks the OpenXR runtime for the recommended size of the image of an eye (xrEnumerateViewConfigurationViews).
  2. VR Player puts it in the frame channel before the program starts (FrameChannel::set_target_eye_size()).
  3. When the program makes its device, the scaler works out the scale from the size the program asked for (ResolutionPolicy::get_fit_scale()): the largest that fits the eye with the program's own proportions, so nothing is stretched.
  4. The back buffer and every render target are made that many times larger.

Keeping the frames even

In a headset two things are felt: how late a picture is, and whether the motion is even. A frame that is shown twice with the one after it skipped is judder, and it is felt even when every frame is fast. The converter and the presenter therefore do three things:

  • they get a frame from the program to the headset with as little work on the program's own thread as they can;
  • they make the program produce one frame for each frame of the headset;
  • they hold the headset's frame for an image that is a few milliseconds late.

The way of a frame

Each frame passes four threads. None waits for the one after it, except that the program waits for the exporter's thread when it has its next frame to hand over.

One frame for each frame of the headset

A program that renders as fast as it can, and a headset that shows frames at a fixed rate, drift against each other: a frame of the program is made that the compositor never takes, or a frame is taken twice. It is felt as judder even when nothing is slow.

With the headset, the presenter therefore tells the program when to make a frame. Each time the compositor asks for one (xrWaitFrame returns) the presenter signals the frame channel (FrameChannel::signal_pace(), a named event). The converter, after the program has presented a frame, waits for that signal (wait_for_pace()) and only then lets the program begin the next one.

PartWhat it does
The delayThe frame is not begun at the signal but a delay after it, so that its images are ready at the right moment. The delay is counted from the signal, not from the moment the program notices it: a frame that has to wait is then not longer by the delay, and one that is late goes on at once. The wait uses a timer fine to a millisecond or less, since a coarser one would be a frame.
The right momentThe compositor builds its layers as soon as it asks for a frame, from the images that are ready by then. The presenter measures how far into a frame of the headset each set of images is ready (HeadsetSink::tune_pace()) and moves the delay until it is ready at 0.3 of a frame. The copy takes more than a frame, so this cannot be worked out beforehand.
Where it cannot hurtA program is not made to wait for a presenter that has not signalled for a quarter of a second (a headset that was taken off), nor longer than 100 ms for one signal. The delay and the signal are in the header of the channel.

An image that is a few milliseconds late

The copy of a frame to the headset takes a good part of a frame and varies, so the images are sometimes ready just after the compositor asks. Without the wait, that frame shows the one before again, and the next shows the new images a frame late: a repeat and a skip for one late copy. The compositor's frame can be ended any time within its period, and it is ended in under a millisecond, so there is room to wait.

Lag and options

The lag of the picture behind what the program did is the work of the four threads plus the margin before the next request: about two frames of the headset. The program's own sound is not delayed, so the picture trails the music by that much. The margin is on purpose; a smaller one gives a shorter lag and more repeats when a copy is late.

  • No preview with the headset. The channel has room for one reader, and the preview's copy of each image costs the same memory bandwidth as the headset's. The window says so; without a headset it previews the images.
  • Pacing can be turned off (--no-pacing on the presenter), to see the difference or for a program whose timing depends on how fast it presents. The VR player always paces.

Vertices in pixels: Direct3D 7 and 8

A program of Direct3D 7 whose vertices are all in pixels is converted by the same pieces with a different renderer. The same CallFeeder follows its calls through the analysis, StereoCore decides each draw from the recipe, and what is done to the vertices is decided by ScreenParallaxPlanner, which the replay uses too, with the bytes moved by Stereo::Core::ScreenParallax.

CallUsed by
StereoCore::make_screen_vertices()Gives an adapter a copy of the vertices moved for the eye.
D3D7EyeRendererUses it for every draw of vertices in pixels.
D3D8EyeRenderer::on_pre_draw()Uses it for DrawPrimitiveUP and DrawIndexedPrimitiveUP draws whose vertex format is XYZRHW (D3D8Frontend::is_pre_transformed_vertex_shader) and makes those draws for both eyes itself. XYZRHW vertices from a vertex buffer end the application with a message (see Stereo).

The second eye

D3D7SurfaceTwins makes a copy of every surface the program renders to (render targets, depth buffers, its back buffer) as the program makes them. For each draw or clear, D3D7EyeRenderer repeats the work on the twins.

The window

When another program shows the images, D3D7WindowedMode has the program render to an invisible window.

  • The cooperative level is that of a window, the display mode is not set, and the surface that is shown has no chain of buffers.
  • The back buffer the program gets is a surface of the converter's own, the size of the display mode times the render scale.
  • The hooks cannot change an argument of Direct3D 7 in place, so the call is not made and the converter makes its own and writes the result where the program is to get it.
  • A flip shows nothing.
  • The window is made fully transparent and click-through by InvisibleWindows (not hidden, since some programs pause while their window is hidden), which also tells the program it is always active, since some programs pause while their window is not.

The resolution

  • The back buffer, its twin and the depth buffer are made as large as the images are shown (the same policy as Direct3D 8; surfaces are capped at 2048 on a side).
  • The viewport, the rectangles of a clear and the positions of the vertices are scaled to match.
  • A program that locks the whole back buffer, writes it and unlocks it (the ending screens of such a program) has it stretched to the whole after the unlock and copied to the twin.
  • A lock to read, or of part of the surface, is only counted.

The images

The back buffer is read before a flip, or before the blit of the surface the program rendered to onto the primary surface (how a program in a window shows a frame). It is converted to B, G, R of 8 bits and put into the channel, the two eyes side by side. When no screen keeps time (no program that shows the images, or one that does not say), the converter holds the program to a frame rate (see the switches).

OpenGL

A program of OpenGL (fixed function, a camera that is the model-view matrix) is converted by the same pieces with GLEyeRenderer.

Which calls draw

  • CallFeeder follows the OpenGL and WGL calls and both SwapBuffers (the one of GDI32 ends a frame like the one of WGL).
  • It takes glCallList, glDrawArrays, glDrawElements and glEnd as the calls that draw.
  • A call can make several draws (a list), so the model builder keeps the cover key and the signature key of each draw that the last call made (get_call_draw_key_vec), and the converter asks StereoCore about each one.
  • A call is made as affected if any of its draws is, otherwise as the class of its draws; a list that mixes interface and scene is counted and told in the log.

The eyes

GLEyeTargets makes two framebuffers of renderbuffers (color, and depth with stencil) and binds the one of the left eye for the application, which never draws to its window, so it does not matter that the window cannot be seen.

  • The functions of framebuffers are found through the wglGetProcAddress that the hooks call through, not the one the application sees: the hooked extension functions have no driver functions behind them until the application makes its context current.
  • The functions of OpenGL 1.1 that the converter calls itself are the driver's too (GLDriverFunctions, from the cached pointers of the hooks), since a hook, besides the callbacks, copies the data of a call (a read of pixels) for a program that watches the application.

A draw

A draw of glDrawArrays, glDrawElements or glCallList is made by the converter for both eyes, and the call of the application is not made.

Class of the drawWhat the converter does
AffectedEach eye gets the matrix of the eye (StereoCore::make_eye_shift_matrix(): half the separation to the side and a shear by the depth, so that what is at the distance of convergence stays put) in front of the model-view matrix the draw has, which is read and put back.
UnaffectedMade for both eyes as it is.
SkippedNot made.
Pushed (see Stereo)Moved by moving the origin of its viewport (PushShiftPlanner::make_viewport; a side-reaching overlay is widened by the shift on each side). For an affected pushed draw the renderer reads the projection and the model-view and adds the parallax of its depth (StereoCore::get_eye_parallax(), plan_push_shift_with_layer()); a projection that is not perspective (DrawMeasurer::is_perspective_projection) says nothing of depth.
  • Any camera is a model-view matrix, so the anchor of the replay (the call that begins the camera) is not needed live.
  • The indices of glDrawElements are the chunk the feeder took out of the interceptor.

A block of glBegin to glEnd

Whether it is affected is known at glEnd, after its vertices were given. The block is therefore compiled to a display list that the converter owns (glNewList at glBegin, glEndList after glEnd) and the list is called for each eye like a draw.

Clears and textures

  • A glClear is made for the right eye as well.
  • What the application renders to a texture (glCopyTexImage2D, glCopyTexSubImage2D) is copied for the right eye, from its framebuffer, to a twin of the texture, made when the first copy is seen with the parameters and the size of the level.
  • The draws of the right eye bind the twin of a texture bound to the active unit, and a glTexParameter call is made to the twin too.
  • A call that cannot be made for the right eye is counted and told in the log, once: pixels drawn with glDrawPixels, glBitmap, glCopyPixels, glAccum, an upload to a texture that has a twin, a display list that is compiled and made at once, a twin bound to a unit that is not the active one.

The images

  • At SwapBuffers GLFrameReader starts reading both eyes from the framebuffers into pixel buffers (as B, G, R, A, the order of the channel), which the graphics card fills while the application goes on, and gives the images of the frame before, from the buffers of the frame before, which are mapped.
  • There are two sets of buffers, and a set is not used again while what was given from it is held (the images carry a token).
  • GLHostMemoryExporter has a thread that turns the rows over (OpenGL has the bottom row first) into the channel, side by side, so that the application's own thread only hands the frame over. A frame that is given while the thread is at the one before is left out.
  • Without a program that shows the images the window shows the left eye (a blit to the back buffer).
  • APISTEREO_DUMP_FRAME, APISTEREO_DUMP_EVERY and APISTEREO_DUMP_FIRST_FRAME write the images of the eyes to a file, as for Direct3D 7. APISTEREO_DUMP_SCENES writes scene images (see the switches below).
  • The framebuffers are made again, at the size of the window, when it changes.

The size

The framebuffers are made as large as the render scale says (see Resolution). What the application states in pixels is made to match by GLResolutionScaler; the call of the application is not made, and the analysis still sees it as it was made.

cache_pixel_reads

An application that samples the picture on a grid reads one pixel more than a thousand times in a row, and each read waits for the graphics card.

  1. The first read of such a run is made as it is.
  2. At the second the graphics card brings the left eye down to the size of the window (a blit that takes for each pixel the one under its centre, into a target of that size which is made once and kept) and it is read once.
  3. The rest of the run is given from that picture (PictureReadbackCache), at scale 1 as well.

Any call that is not such a read ends the run, since it may change the picture; an application that has set the way pixels are packed, or a buffer for them, is read as it is.

Refused calls

  • Any other read of pixels (more than one pixel, the depth, floats, other colours) or drawing pixels at places in pixels (glReadPixels, glDrawPixels, glBitmap, glCopyPixels, glPixelZoom).
  • Asking for the viewport, the scissor box, the width of lines or the size of points.
  • Asking for the size of a level of a texture.
  • Giving pixels to a texture that it renders to, and copying part of the framebuffer to a texture that it gave pixels.

Comfort

With measure_depth in the comfort of the recipe, GLEyeRenderer reads the depth buffer of the left eye before a pass is cleared and at the end of the frame.

Measuring

The read is of a copy of the depth buffer that the graphics card makes small. DepthBufferStatistics turns the depths into distances with the projection of the pass; the nearest and farthest surfaces are the edges of what was drawn, and what was not drawn is the sky, as far as the far plane.

DepthMeasurer gives the same numbers as DepthBufferStatistics::measure() to the last bit.

  • On a processor with AVX2 (found out at its start; depth_measurer_avx2.cpp in libs/stereo/core) it counts the distances in a histogram, the bins being the top bits of the distance as a float, with the pixels taken eight at a time, and picks the three distances among those of three bins only.
  • Without AVX2 the scalar measure is used.

Lag

  • The read is made to a buffer of the graphics card, which the converter does not wait for: the result is taken at the end of the frame after it.
  • FrameDepthLedger puts the passes of a frame together (the farthest, the nearest, the median of their medians), so the depth that is given is that of the frame before, with the scene key of that frame (GLEyeRenderer::get_depth_lag() says 1; with no buffer of the graphics card to read to, the depth is read at once and the lag is 0).
  • A frame with no depth in it is left to what was baked for its shape.

Convergence by time

  • The nearest and farthest of the passes go to StereoCore::on_frame_depth(), which has the eyes converge and the separation limited as RecipeApplier::get_comfort_limits() says (the function that bakes the limits of a scene).
  • The eyes follow that by the time that goes (ConvergenceModel::get_fraction_of_the_way: 5 percent of the way in the time of a frame at 72 Hz, a time constant of about 0.27 s, the same at any rate; the converter measures how long each frame took), so a run that is not held to a rate sees the same adaptation as the headset does.
  • The scene dump counts frames, so a run that writes scenes is held to a rate (see the switches).

Attached draws

The decision is StereoCore::is_last_draw_attached_to_camera(). A draw whose origin is nearer the camera than camera_attached_depth of the convergence distance is made as it is for both eyes, unless one of these holds:

  • the recipe names it in eye_space_cover_keys;
  • the recipe has comfort.automatic_layers and the baker found it to be in eye space (its model-view is the identity because the application put its vertices in the space of the eye, so the origin says nothing of where they are).

Not followed

  • Matrices that are loaded inside a display list the application compiles (the camera of a list is then not the one that the eye matrix is put before).
  • A twin that is bound to a unit that is not the active one (counted).
  • A second context (it is not converted).

The frame channel

Shared memory between the 32-bit program and the 64-bit presenter, named from the session (Local\APIStereo_<session>).

  • Images start at multiples of 64 KB and rows at multiples of 256 bytes, so the GPU can import the memory as a heap.
  • The two sides say how they can pass images (a mask of transport kinds), and the producer uses the best that both can; host memory is the one kind there is.
  • The floats of the controls are stored as bits next to a has-value flag (read_float / write_float), so a reader never takes a value that was not set.

Three slots, nobody waits

There are three image slots: the producer writes one that is neither the newest nor the one being read, so nobody waits.

The log and the switches

The log is off unless asked for (--log on the launcher and the presenter, which VRPlayer passes on): then %TEMP%\apistereo_<session>.log has statistics each second, as count (n), mean, worst and how many were late, in milliseconds, and every 600 frames one line of the converter's counts (draws affected, unaffected and skipped, unknown draws and frames, matrices kept, twins, and how long the callbacks took a frame, and how much of it in the draws). Off, only what has to be said is written: why the converter stops the application, a frame that cannot be passed on, a headset that cannot be used.

Reading the log

  • If the program's presents are even and the repeats are many, the delay is ours.
  • Seconds in which the program itself stalls (a scene that loads) show as fewer presents and many repeats, and nothing downstream can show images the program has not made.
  • With --log --wait the launcher reads the last statistics line when the application exits and says what the run came to: frames seen and how many draws were affected, unaffected and skipped, and a line for each thing the converter could not do (draws of a signature the recipe does not know, frames of a scene it has no distance for, surfaces that could not be given a twin, calls not made for the right eye).
