Replay

Executing the calls of a workload again, without the program. Here it is done for both eyes: it is where recipes are tried, where a frame is checked to be drawable twice, and the proving ground for what the live converter does inside a running program.

The engine

The replay engine belongs to API Interceptor (ReplayEngineCore, a static library; see its documentation). It walks the calls of a workload one after the other with an executor for each API, and knows nothing of stereo.

What this project adds goes in through two interfaces of the engine:

  • an IFrameExecutor, which plays the calls of a frame (the engine presents the frame and pauses between two);
  • the executors of the calls, which a class can derive from.

The result is StereoReplayEngineDLL. A program that wants to hear of frames and calls derives from NullReplayObserver, an observer that does nothing, and overrides what it cares about (the player's FrameRangePlayer, the inspector's LastFramePauser).

Direct3D 7's executor replays a program in a window whatever it did:

  • the primary surface shows the window;
  • the back buffer of a flipping chain is a surface of the replay;
  • a flip is a blit to the window.

Two eyes

With a recipe, the engine executes each frame twice, the first time for the left eye and the second for the right. The two images go to a sink, which is how the player shows them. Four classes of this project do the work:

ClassWhat it does
RecipeApplierDecides, for each draw, whether it is drawn once, for each eye, or not at all, and whether it is pushed (the next section).
EyeMatrixBinderBinds the matrices of the eye to the draws that are drawn per eye, by the rules of Stereo. A kept matrix is made again when the eye separation or the convergence distance changes.
FrameRewriterRewrites the calls that set a matrix which a rule changes, before the frame is run. An eye_convergence rule gets the convergence distance of the scene of the frame, as in the live converter.
StereoFrameExecutorReads a frame, runs it once for each eye and gives the images to the sink.

What a frame gets depends on what the recipe says of it:

FrameWhat the replay does
Of a shape the recipe knows (or a recipe without known_shape_keys)Executed twice, for the left and for the right eye.
Of another shape, with on_unknown_frame monoExecuted twice as it is: both eyes get the same image.
Of another shape, with skipNot played.
Of another shape, with failThe replay stops and says the shape key.
That carries the picture over from the frame beforeEach eye keeps a picture of its own.
That carries over another resource, which the recipe does not list in per_eye_texturesThe replay stops and names the resource: two eyes cannot share it.

Deciding a draw

The replay and the live converter ask the same function, RecipeApplier::decide_draw, about each draw. The answer is a class (affected, unaffected or skipped) and whether the draw is pushed.

StepRule
NamesThe lists of the recipe: skipped first, then the most specific name in affected and unaffected (Naming draws). A draw named nowhere is affected. pushed says whether it is pushed, and so does comfort.push_unaffected for every draw that is left alone.
Baked flat layerWith comfort.automatic_layers, a draw whose cover key is in flat_layer_draws is unaffected, even if affected names it.
Cover-key holdA draw pushed by its cover key (pushed.cover_keys, part_cover_keys, and overlay_draws with the automatic layers) is pushed and unaffected: drawn once for both eyes.
Waiver in the replayThe replay gives the shift of the eye to a camera, so every draw that shares the camera gets it (OpenGL, below). A draw pushed by its cover key that shares its camera with an affected draw is therefore not held: the rules apply to it. The live converter has no waiver.

A baked flat layer has no waiver: one that shares its camera with an affected draw is the second case that stops the replay in the camera.

Putting the state back

A frame may rely on what the frame before it left on the device (a render target not set because the last frame set it). The first execution changes that, so the second would start somewhere else, unless the state is put back.

APIHow the state is put back
Direct3D 8save_state and restore_state are a state block.
Direct3D 7The executor keeps the render states, texture stage states, transforms, viewport, render target and stage textures that the replayed calls set, and sets them again.
OpenGLGLStateSaver; see the next section.

Only the ways of the inspector that ask for it put the state back (restore and recipe); twice shows what a frame does without it. The player puts it back when it renders both eyes.

OpenGL: what is made once, what twice

The GL executor (GLCallExecutor) says when each call is made in a frame that is executed twice:

CallsMade inWhy
What makes an object (glGen*, glCreate*)the first execution onlyA second one would make others, and the names that the replay maps would be lost.
What deletes one (glDelete*) and the swap of the buffersthe last execution onlyThe objects and the image are needed until the second eye is done.
Everything else (state, matrices, draws, uploads, the lists)each executionEach eye needs them.

The state is put back between the executions by GLStateSaver. It saves two things:

  • the attribute stacks (glPushAttrib and glPushClientAttrib with all the bits);
  • the matrices, which the stacks do not hold: the model-view and the projection matrix, and the texture matrix of each unit, as numbers.

What the objects hold is not put back. A frame executed twice comes out as the same frame executed once (--ways checks it).

OpenGL: the camera

A recipe with a view_anchor_shift (see Recipes) gives the eyes parallax without a rule for a matrix: GL has none to rewrite, for the camera is built by calls on the model-view matrix.

The front end says, for each draw, the call that begins its camera: the anchor, the last load of that matrix that its stack descends from.

  • A push copies it with the matrix.
  • A pop gives back the one of the matrix below.
  • What is multiplied in leaves it.
Anchor callPlayed, for an affected draw, as
glLoadIdentitya load of the shift of the eye
glLoadMatrixf, glLoadMatrixda load of the shift times the matrix of the program

Three cases stop the replay with a message that says which:

  • a call that is the anchor of draws of which some are affected and some are not;
  • a draw that is affected and has no anchor;
  • an anchor that is not a glLoadIdentity, glLoadMatrixf or glLoadMatrixd.

The message names the draws. The recipe then names them in unaffected, or says "draws_without_anchor": "leave", which plays the second kind as it is. A recipe of this kind for an OpenGL program is in apps/recipes.

OpenGL: the textures

A texture that rendering fills, and that a frame reads what an earlier frame left in, has a copy for each eye (per_eye_textures).

StepWhat happens
Making the copyStereoGLCallExecutor makes it when the texture is first changed after the analysis knows what it is (a texture has no resource before it is given a size), with the parameters of the texture.
NamingFrom then on the name that the program has is mapped to the copy of the eye, and what each unit has bound is that copy.
A call that changes the textureIn a frame executed once it is made for the other copy too; in one executed twice each execution makes it for its own eye.
DeletingThe copies are deleted together.

A texture that is not carried from frame to frame is shared and needs none of this. For such a program, making its render target a texture for each eye anyway gives the images that one texture gives.

Pushed draws and vertices in pixels

  • Pushed draws (OpenGL). A draw that is pushed is moved by a glViewport that the executor plays before its first call (execute_viewport_call), with the origin shifted by PushShiftPlanner. The viewport it moves is the one the device has at that moment (glGetIntegerv, through IEyeExecutor::read_viewport), so a program that sets none is moved from the viewport of the window of the replay, not from the size in the call that created the window. The call of the draw puts back the viewport that was read.
  • Side-reaching draws. One that the baker lists in side_reaching_draws is also widened by the shift on each side (PushShiftPlanner::make_viewport), so that no edge is bare.
  • Vertices in pixels. A program whose vertices no matrix moves has no matrix to give the eyes. The engine moves the x of each vertex of an affected Direct3D 7 draw before it is played (D3D7DrawRewriter, using Stereo::Core::ScreenSpaceVertexShifter), by the screen-space formula.
  • Textures and flat quads. A draw into a texture is moved like any other, so the texture is the picture of the eye; the quad that draws the texture to the screen is flat and stays.
  • Pushed pre-transformed draws. A pushed Direct3D 7 draw to what is presented, with overlay_parallax set, has every x moved by the same number of pixels (D3D7DrawRewriter::rewrite_by).

Seeking

There is no way to jump into the middle of a workload: the state of the device is made by every call before it. A seek is a replay up to the frame, which for a long capture is long. The dependencies make most of it unnecessary.

SeekWhat the replay does
AheadHurries through the frames before the target: unpaced, not presented, not read back.
Back in timeStarts the replay over and hurries to the target.
With the dependenciesPlays the frames that nothing after the target reads in part only (the figure).

What it needs and who does it

PartWhat it is
.depsThe dependencies of the workload (APIDumpDependencyExtractor).
.indexWhere the segments are, and a state frame of each (below). Kept and made by Analysis::APIDumpIndex::WorkloadIndex: StereoReplayInspector --index makes it, and so do the replay player and the slicer when they need it. Without it every segment is read in full.
FrameSlicePlannerIn Analysis; makes the plan.
SliceSkipperCarries the plan out. It hands each call to the handler of its API (ISliceCallHandler): ContentSliceCallHandler for Direct3D 7 and 8, GLSliceCallHandler for OpenGL.
HurriedFrameSelectorRuns the frames before the target unpaced.
  • Correctness is checked rather than assumed: with every rule below, the images of the target frame of a seek (both eyes with a recipe) are byte for byte those of a replay without it. The inspector's screenshots, with --deps and without, are how to check it.
  • A capture where every frame reads what the previous one wrote gains nothing: no frame is dead.
  • Frames are counted as the converter counts them live (the window of a running converter says Frame N), so a frame seen there can be typed here.

State frames in the index

The index and what is in it belong to the analysis (src/libs/analysis/api_dump_index, namespace Analysis::APIDumpIndex), not to the interceptor: the replay engine does not read the index, its host does. The stereo replay engine loads it the first time a seek needs it, and gives the engine where the segments start (set_segment_starts). A segment whose frames are all dead is not decompressed from the workload. The .index holds, for each segment, a state frame: the calls of the segment that a dead frame still makes, in the layout of the workload, with the data chunks they use. The reader takes such a segment from it, so a seek skips the decompression of most of the file. The calls it keeps are still filtered as before (the attribute pushes of OpenGL), so the images are the same.

PartWhat it does
CallDescriberSetAsks the describer of the API of each call whether it is content, state or conditional (CallClass). One describer each for OpenGL, Direct3D 7 and Direct3D 8; a call of any other API is state.
StateSegmentBuilderWrites the state frame of a segment when the index is made.
WorkloadReaderReads a segment from its state frame when state_only_func says its frames are wanted as state only.

An index of an older version is not used (every segment is read in full) until it is made again: StereoReplayInspector --index does. It is about 1 % of the workload (53 MB for 4.4 GB).

The calls of a state frame are coalesced when the index is made. A dead frame makes no content, so nothing reads the state that its calls set until a call that reads state; of the calls that set the same piece of state (a capability, a client array, a texture parameter), only the last before such a call is kept. Each call says what it does to state (StateEffect): it assigns a piece of state, selects what later calls act on (the active texture unit, the matrix mode), is ordered (made in place, such as a matrix call), is a barrier (a context, a display list, a pushed attribute), or is recorded into a display list. The calls that a kept call needs are made again where they are needed (a copy of the call that chose the unit), and the selectors are as they were at every barrier, frame and segment end.

PartWhat it does
IStateEffectDescriberOne for each API: OpenGL (an entry for each function that has one, and for every other function that GLFunctionClassifier knows: queries and names change no state, any other call is a barrier), the window system (GDI32, USER32 and WGL: all barriers) and Direct3D 7 and 8 (not coalesced: all barriers).
StateCoalescerChooses the calls to make, in order.
StateCoalescerVerifierPlays the original calls and the chosen ones on a model of state, and compares what every call that reads state would see.

The index is made to fail rather than to guess. A function that no describer names ends the process with its number (add it to the table of the describer, saying what it does to state), as does a call of an API that has none, a call without the number that tells what it sets, a display list that is made and executed at once, and any difference that the verifier finds. When in doubt, a function is a barrier: it is made as it was. The tables are checked by tests, among them tens of thousands of random sequences of calls.

OpenGL: what a dead frame leaves out

A dead frame leaves out its content calls and makes the rest: the state, the textures and the lists. For Direct3D the content calls are draws, clears, copies (Blt, CopyRects, UpdateTexture) and the locks and unlocks of buffers and surfaces. For OpenGL they are these:

KindCalls
VerticesglBegin to glEnd with the vertices between them (glVertex*, glRect*, glArrayElement)
Array drawsglDrawArrays, glDrawElements, glDrawRangeElements, glMultiDrawArrays, glMultiDrawElements
Clears, copies, readsglClear, glCopyTexImage2D, glCopyTexSubImage2D, glReadPixels
PixelsglDrawPixels, glBitmap
ListsglCallList, decided per list (the next table)

A seek to frame 20000 of one OpenGL capture (4.4 GB, 27,000 frames) takes 2.5 s instead of 29.3 s. The display lists need care:

CaseRule
Calls of a list that is being made (glNewList to glEndList)Always made, so that the list has them.
A call of a list that only drawsLeft out. GLDisplayListTracker follows the lists as the calls come and classes them as the analysis does (GLListEyeDependence).
A call of a list that sets stateMade, since the frames after it continue from what it leaves.
A call of a list that is about the eye: it loads or moves a matrix, makes something relative to it, sets a projection, a viewport or a scissor box, calls such a list, or is not definedEnds the process with a message (AI_ASSERT_ALWAYS), since leaving it out would leave the matrices of the frames after it not as the capture had them.
The content calls of a dead frameThe reader does not make them into calls at all (SliceSkipper::get_call_kept_func_factory). The calls of a list are left to the skipper, which knows the list. The data chunks of OpenGL calls are still read: a list that is being made may need them, and the filter cannot tell.
A segmentRead by one thread, which follows glNewList and glEndList in it, so the calls of a list that is being made are kept. A list that is made across two segments ends the process the same way.

More rules of the OpenGL replay

RuleWhat it does
ReadbacksThe replay does not make glGetFloatv, nor glReadPixels when no pixel pack buffer is bound. They only write into the memory of the application; what the application computed of them is in the calls that follow, as dumped. A second eye needs them as little.
Texture parametersA glTexParameteri or glTexParameterf that gives a parameter of a texture the value it has is not made (GLTextureParameterCache, an interceptor of the engine). A driver takes as long for it as for a call that changes something, and a workload sets the same few parameters again and again. It is given the calls of OpenGL and WGL only.
Textures filled a part at a timeThe way back of a frame that uses such a texture ends earlier (see below).
State that a pop undoesThe reader leaves out the calls that only set state a pop undoes (see below).

Texture parameters: what the cache knows

  • Only the ten parameters that the twin of a texture for the other eye takes are looked at (the filters, how the coordinates wrap, the levels and their generation), so a call that is left out is one that the twin would not have changed either.
  • The first call that gives a parameter its value is always made, since what the texture has until then is not known.
  • What the cache knows is forgotten, not guessed, when a call may change it without being told:
    • a display list that sets the texture state (or is not known) is called;
    • textures are deleted, or glPopAttrib is called;
    • a context is made or deleted;
    • a call that another interceptor left out;
    • a replay starts over.

Textures filled a part at a time

A texture whose writes all say where they landed (written_extent) is whole again once the frames that wrote it have written all of the part that any write reaches. The versions before those do not show in it.

  • The way back of a frame that uses it ends at the last version that replaced all of it, which holds what the writes do not reach (FrameSlicePlanner, compute_windows; at most 64 versions are looked at together).
  • A frame that is made for a version it wrote is made in part: the replay makes all of its calls, but only what flows into that version (input_resource_vec) is looked for in the frames before it.
  • The draws that sample the texture after the copy to it use the texture, and what they render is not read by any frame that is made.
  • Example: a texture of 1024 x 512 to which 845 frames copy the same 147 x 414 rectangle. A seek into them makes 3 of the frames before the target instead of 739.

State that a pop undoes

In a dead frame, between a glPushAttrib and its glPopAttrib, the reader also leaves out the calls that only give a piece of state a value which the groups of the mask save. Nothing that is made looks at the state before the pop does away with it.

GLAttributeCoverage says which calls a mask covers: capabilities, materials, lights, the texture environment and generation, blend, depth, polygon and line state, the current color, normal and texture coordinates, and the like. What is made anyway:

Made anywayWhy
The push and the popThey bound the pair.
The calls that choose where the next ones go (the active texture unit, the matrix mode, the bound texture)Later calls depend on the choice.
The parameters of texturesThey change an object, which the pop does not undo.
The state of the client (the arrays and their pointers)glPushClientAttrib saves it and glPushAttrib does not.
  • A frame that is left out has to pop what it pushes: one that ends first ends the process with a message (AI_ASSERT_ALWAYS), since the state that it would have left is not made.
  • A pair that begins in a segment and ends in the next leaves out what follows the push in the first one only.
  • Example: a program that pushes all of the attributes once in each frame, and every one of whose dead frames pops them. --redundant-state says how many calls of each kind a seek does not make.

Where they are

In the release (see Building) the engine and the two programs are in tools\stereo_replay\, each as _x64 and _x86. A 64-bit player starts the 32-bit one for a 32-bit workload that has Direct3D 7, Direct3D 8 or OpenGL calls, which are the captures of 32-bit programs. In the source they are in src/tools/stereo_replay, in engine_dll, player and inspector.

What the inspector is for

The inspector is the test that the engine is fit for stereo: that a replay is repeatable, and that a frame executed twice draws the same as once. It is also where everything else about a recipe is tried without a window.

The ways of replaying

The check replays frames A to B once for each way. The frames as they are come first and are always replayed; every other way is compared with them.

Way--ways nameEach frame is executedState put back
As they arealwaysas the program made it, once-
As they are, againagainas they are, again: how much a replay differs from itself-
Twicetwicetwiceno
Twice, the state put back betweenrestoretwiceyes
Twice, for the eyes of the reciperecipe, needs --recipetwice: left eye, then right eyeyes
  • Without --ways the ways are again, then twice and restore, or with a recipe recipe alone.
  • --ways again,restore names others, in the order to replay them. Each way is a replay of the workload from its start, so fewer ways are less to wait for.
  • --parallel replays the ways at the same time, each with an engine of its own: faster, and it needs the memory of all.
  • The last way is the one under test. The earlier ones are printed and show how much a replay differs from itself, but only the last decides the exit code and the dump.

The overlay report

--overlay-report candidates.json decides, for each draw that may be a 2D overlay, whether it really makes pixels. Some of the frames of each draw (the list says which: its first, middle and last frame) are replayed with the draw and without it (the recipe skips it in its pass), and the images are compared.

VerdictMeaning
Makes pixelsThe two images differ. An animated GIF flips between the frame and the frame with the pixels the draw makes in magenta, and a still is written.
overdrawn_laterThe frame cut after the draw's last call differs with and without the draw: what is drawn after it covers its pixels.
not_rasterizedEven the cut frame is the same: the draw is transparent, clipped, outside the picture or hidden by the depth test.
pass_makes_no_pixelsLeaving out the whole pass changes nothing. One replay of the pass says it for all of its draws in the frame.
  • The run writes the animations and stills to --dump-dir, a candidates.json with the number of draws that make pixels, and an index.html.
  • A draw that the recipe or the converter leaves alone gets a flag when it is deep (its vertices are at many depths) or changes more than 40% of the picture.
  • The page lists the draws on the left, each with a dot for every frame it was looked at in. A click goes to the draw on the right, where its frames come one after the other in the order of time, so that how it behaves over the run can be seen.
OptionEffect
--overlay-report-last-frame NLeaves out the frames after the Nth (each replay goes from the start to its frame, so the later frames cost most).
--jobs NThe work is shared between N processes (1 to 63; 1 by default: none). A replay is bound by the bandwidth of the memory, so more processes may not be faster.
--depsThe candidates of a frame are played in one run of the frame (FrameVariantReplayPlan); see below.
--overlay-report-exactReplays one candidate at a time, for all of them, as a check of the one-run play (the images are the same).
--overlay-report-shard-entries NA process looks at no more than N candidates (40 by default; 0: no limit). A longer list is split into more parts than --jobs, and the parts run no more than --jobs at a time.
--overlay-report-shard I N, --overlay-report-result FILEThe report gives these to the processes it starts: part I of N, writing what it found to the file. Not for a person.

The one-run play replays the frame once for each variant (as it is, each pass left out, each draw left out, the cut frames), each from the state that the frame before left. The frame's dependencies must allow it: it makes or releases no resource, and what it writes it replaces before it reads it. A frame that does not allow it, or one that fails so, is replayed a candidate at a time.

The parts exist because a replay leaves some memory behind (1 to 5 MB, more in the later frames) and a 32-bit process runs out of address space after a few hundred.

Where the images come from

The images that the modes compare come from the executor of each API, after each execution of a frame and before the frame is presented.

OpenGLThe color buffer of the context that is current: the whole client area of its window, read as the back buffer after the swap.
  • The specification leaves the content of the buffer undefined after a swap; on the drivers the replay is run with, it is what was presented.
  • The rows are put from the top and the alpha is 255, whatever the window has.
  • The swap of the buffers of a window is made after the executions of a frame, as the present of Direct3D is, so that the image of a frame is read before it.
  • A draw that the recipe leaves out is left out whole: the calls that come before the call of the draw and belong to it (the glBegin and the vertices of a block that glEnd makes a draw of) go with it, as a primitive is drawn when its vertices are given.
  • The draws of a list that is called share the call of the list, so they are left out together or not at all.

The inspector's source has a file for each of these ways of working (replay_runner, comparison, screenshot_mode, profile_mode, redundant_state_counter_mode, overlay_report_mode), a command_line that has tests, and a main.cpp that loads the recipe and chooses. replay_runner holds the table of the ways (ReplayWay).
