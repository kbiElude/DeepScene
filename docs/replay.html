Replay

Executing the calls of a workload again, without the program. Here it is done for both eyes: it is where recipes are tried, where a frame is checked to be drawable twice, and the proving ground for what the live converter does inside a running program.

The engine

The replay engine belongs to API Interceptor (ReplayEngineCore, a static library; see its documentation). It walks the calls of a workload one after the other with an executor for each API, and knows nothing of stereo.

What this project adds goes in through two interfaces of the engine:

  • an IFrameExecutor, which plays the calls of a frame (the engine presents the frame and pauses between two);
  • the executors of the calls, which a class can derive from.

The result is StereoReplayEngineDLL. A program that wants to hear of frames and calls derives from NullReplayObserver, an observer that does nothing, and overrides what it cares about (the player's FrameRangeObserver, the checker's StopObserver).

Direct3D 7's executor replays a program in a window whatever it did:

  • the primary surface shows the window;
  • the back buffer of a flipping chain is a surface of the replay;
  • a flip is a blit to the window.

Two eyes

With a recipe, the engine executes every frame twice. The recipe tells, for each draw, whether it is drawn once, for each eye, or not at all (RecipeApplier), and the matrices of the eye are bound to the draws that are drawn per eye (EyeMatrixBinder), by the rules of Stereo. A frame is rewritten (FrameRewriter) before it is run, and the two images go to a sink, which is how the player shows them.

Putting the state back

A frame may rely on what the frame before it left on the device (a render target not set because the last frame set it). The first execution changes that, so the second would start somewhere else, unless the state is put back.

APIHow the state is put back
Direct3D 8save_state and restore_state are a state block.
Direct3D 7The executor keeps the render states, texture stage states, transforms, viewport, render target and stage textures that the replayed calls set, and sets them again.
OpenGLGLStateSaver; see the next section.

OpenGL: what is made once, what twice

The GL executor (GLCallExecutor) says when each call is made in a frame that is executed twice:

CallsMade inWhy
What makes an object (glGen*, glCreate*)the first execution onlyA second one would make others, and the names that the replay maps would be lost.
What deletes one (glDelete*) and the swap of the buffersthe last execution onlyThe objects and the image are needed until the second eye is done.
Everything else (state, matrices, draws, uploads, the lists)each executionEach eye needs them.

The state is put back between the executions by GLStateSaver. It saves two things:

  • the attribute stacks (glPushAttrib and glPushClientAttrib with all the bits);
  • the matrices, which the stacks do not hold: the model-view and the projection matrix, and the texture matrix of each unit, as numbers.

What the objects hold is not put back. A frame executed twice comes out as the same frame executed once (--ways checks it).

OpenGL: the camera

A recipe with a view_anchor_shift (see Recipes) gives the eyes parallax without a rule for a matrix: GL has none to rewrite, for the camera is built by calls on the model-view matrix.

The front end says, for each draw, the call that begins its camera: the anchor, the last load of that matrix that its stack descends from.

  • A push copies it with the matrix.
  • A pop gives back the one of the matrix below.
  • What is multiplied in leaves it.
Anchor callPlayed, for an affected draw, as
glLoadIdentitya load of the shift of the eye
glLoadMatrixf, glLoadMatrixda load of the shift times the matrix of the program

Two cases stop the replay with a message that says which:

  • a call that is the anchor of draws of which some are affected and some are not;
  • a draw that is affected and has no anchor.

The recipe then leaves the draws alone, or says "draws_without_anchor": "leave". An OpenGL program is played executed twice with a recipe of this kind (apps/recipes).

OpenGL: the textures

A texture that rendering fills, and that a frame reads what an earlier frame left in, has a copy for each eye (per_eye_textures).

StepWhat happens
Making the copyStereoGLCallExecutor makes it when the texture is first changed after the analysis knows what it is (a texture has no resource before it is given a size), with the parameters of the texture.
NamingFrom then on the name that the program has is mapped to the copy of the eye, and what each unit has bound is that copy.
A call that changes the textureIn a frame executed once it is made for the other copy too; in one executed twice each execution makes it for its own eye.
DeletingThe copies are deleted together.

A texture that is not carried from frame to frame is shared and needs none of this. For such a program, making its render target a texture for each eye anyway gives the images that one texture gives.

Pushed draws and vertices in pixels

  • Pushed draws. A draw that the recipe names in pushed is moved by a glViewport that the executor plays before its first call (execute_viewport_call), with the origin shifted by PushShiftPlanner. One that the baker lists in side_reaching_draws is widened by the shift on each side (PushShiftPlanner::make_viewport), so that no edge is bare.
  • Baked flat layers. A draw that is a baked flat layer (RecipeApplier::is_cover_flat_layer, with comfort.automatic_layers) is left alone whatever its structure is called, and does not give its camera the shift of the eye.
  • Vertices in pixels. A program whose vertices no matrix moves has no matrix to give the eyes. The engine moves the x of each vertex of an affected Direct3D 7 draw before it is played (D3D7DrawRewriter, using Stereo::Core::ScreenParallax), by the screen-space formula. A draw into a texture is moved like any other, so the texture is the picture of the eye; the quad that draws the texture to the screen is flat and stays.

Seeking

There is no way to jump into the middle of a workload: the state of the device is made by every call before it. A frame back in time starts the replay over; a frame ahead is reached by hurrying through the frames before it, unpaced, not presented and not read back. A seek is then as long as the replay up to the frame, which for a long capture is long. The dependencies make most of it unnecessary.

What it needs and who does it

PartWhat it is
.depsThe dependencies of the workload (APIDumpDependencyExtractor).

FrameSlicePlannerIn Analysis; makes the plan.
SliceSkipperCarries the plan out.
FrameHurryRuns the frames before the target unpaced.
  • Correctness is checked rather than assumed: with every rule below, the images of the target frame of a seek (both eyes with a recipe) are byte for byte those of a replay without it. The checker's screenshots, with --deps and without, are how to check it.
  • A capture where every frame reads what the previous one wrote gains nothing: no frame is dead.
  • Frames are counted as the converter counts them live (the window of a running converter says Frame N), so a frame seen there can be typed here.

OpenGL: what a dead frame leaves out

A dead frame leaves out these calls: glBegin to glEnd with the vertices between them, glClear, the array draws, glReadPixels, glCopyTexImage2D and glCopyTexSubImage2D, glDrawPixels, glBitmap and glCallList. The state, the textures and the lists are made. A seek to frame 20000 of one OpenGL capture takes 16.4 s instead of 30.7 s.

CaseRule
Calls of a list that is being made (glNewList to glEndList)Always made, so that the list has them.
A call of a listLeft out only if the list does draws and state and nothing of the eye's own. GLDisplayListTracker follows the lists as the calls come and classes them as the analysis does (GLListEyeDependence).
A list that loads or moves a matrix, sets a projection, a viewport or a scissor box, calls such a list, or is not definedEnds the process with a message (AI_ASSERT_ALWAYS), since leaving it out would leave the matrices of the frames after it not as the capture had them.
The content calls of a dead frameThe reader does not make them into calls at all (SliceSkipper::get_call_kept_func_factory), and the chunks that only those calls refer to are left out. The calls of a list are left to the skipper, which knows the list.
A segmentRead by one thread, which follows glNewList and glEndList in it, so the calls of a list that is being made are kept. A list that is made across two segments ends the process the same way.

More rules of the OpenGL replay

RuleWhat it does
ReadbacksThe replay does not make glGetFloatv, nor glReadPixels when no pixel pack buffer is bound: they only write into the memory of the application, what the app computed of them is in the calls that follow as it was dumped, and a replay has no use for the values (so a second eye needs them as little).
Texture parametersA glTexParameteri or glTexParameterf that gives a parameter of a texture the value it has is not made (GLTextureParameterCache, one of the interceptors of the engine): a driver takes such a call as long as one that changes something, and a workload sets the same few parameters again and again.
Textures filled a part at a timeThe way back of a frame that uses such a texture ends earlier (see below).
State that a pop undoesThe reader leaves out the calls that only set state a pop undoes (see below).

Texture parameters: what the cache knows

  • Only the ten parameters that the twin of a texture for the other eye takes are looked at (the filters, how the coordinates wrap, the levels and their generation), so a call that is left out is one that the twin would not have changed either.
  • The first call that gives a parameter its value is always made, since what the texture has until then is not known.
  • What the cache knows is forgotten, not guessed, when a call may change it without being told: a display list that sets the texture state (or is not known) is called, textures are deleted, glPopAttrib, a context is made or deleted, a call that another interceptor left out, and a replay that starts over.

Textures filled a part at a time

A texture whose writes all say where they landed (written_extent) is whole again once the frames that wrote it have written all of the part that any write reaches. The versions before those do not show in it.

  • The way back of a frame that uses it ends there (FrameSlicePlanner, compute_windows; at most 64 versions are looked at together), at the last version that replaced all of it, which holds what the writes do not reach.
  • A frame that is made for a version it wrote is made in part: the replay makes all of its calls, but only what flows into that version (input_resource_vec) is looked for in the frames before it. The draws that sample the texture after the copy to it use the texture, and what they render is not read by any frame that is made.
  • Example: a texture of 1024 x 512 to which 845 frames copy the same 147 x 414 rectangle. A seek into them makes 3 of the frames before the target instead of 739.

State that a pop undoes

In a dead frame, between a glPushAttrib and its glPopAttrib, the reader also leaves out the calls that only give a piece of state a value which the groups of the mask save (GLAttributeCoverage: capabilities, materials, lights, the texture environment and generation, blend, depth, polygon and line state, the current color, normal and texture coordinates, and the like), since nothing that is made looks at the state before the pop does away with it.

Made anywayWhy
The push and the popThey bound the pair.
The calls that choose where the next ones go (the active texture unit, the matrix mode, the bound texture)Later calls depend on the choice.
The parameters of texturesThey change an object, which the pop does not undo.
The state of the client (the arrays and their pointers)glPushClientAttrib saves it and glPushAttrib does not.
  • A frame that is left out has to pop what it pushes: one that ends first ends the process with a message (AI_ASSERT_ALWAYS), as the state that it would have left is not made.
  • A pair that begins in a segment and ends in the next leaves out what follows the push in the first one only.
  • Example: a program that pushes all of the attributes once in each frame, and every one of whose dead frames pops them. --state-census says how many calls of each kind a seek does not make.

Where they are

In the release (see Building) the engine and the two programs are in tools\stereo_replay\, each as _x64 and _x86. A 64-bit player starts the 32-bit one for a 32-bit workload, and the 32-bit ones replay the Direct3D 7, Direct3D 8 and OpenGL captures, which are of 32-bit programs. In the source they are in src/tools/stereo_replay, in engine_dll, player and consistency_checker.

What the checker is for

The checker is the test that the engine is fit for stereo: that a replay is repeatable, and that a frame executed twice draws the same as once. It is also where everything else about a recipe is tried without a window.

The modes

ModeSays
(none) with --ways, --parallelReplays the frames as they are, again, executed twice, with the state put back, and for the eyes of the recipe; hashes each frame's image and compares the ways. It can write the images of the first frame that differs, with an image of the pixels that differ. Each way is a replay from the start; --parallel replays them at the same time, each with an engine of its own.
--from A, --to B, --dump-frame N, --dump-dir DIR, --eye-separation D, --recipe FILEOptions for the modes. --from and --to bound the frames (0 to 999 by default). --dump-dir is where the images of the first frame in which an execution differs go (as PNG files); --dump-frame writes the images of frame N there as well. --recipe replays the frames twice with the recipe, the first execution for the left eye and the second for the right; --eye-separation is the distance between the eyes for it (0 by default).
--screenshot N --out f.pngOne frame as a PNG: with a recipe both eyes side by side (or --eye left, right, both, anaglyph), without one the frame as it is. Frames before N are played once for the left eye unless --history is given; --deps makes it quick and exact.
--contact-sheet K --out f.pngOne PNG of K frames spread evenly over a range, each as a screenshot is.
--same-frames-as other.workloadWhether two workloads give the same images (exit code 3 if not): how a slice is checked against its workload.
--profileWhere the time of the calls went: the functions and the frames that took longest.
--state-censusWhat the calls of OpenGL in the frames that a seek to frame B does not make (--deps says which) do to the state of the context: per function, how many give a piece of state the value it has, and how many are overwritten by another call before a frame that is made, a push or a pop of attributes, a call of a list or another barrier looks at it; and whether each of those frames pushes and pops all that it changes.
--overlay-report candidates.jsonOf the draws that may be 2D overlays (RecipeBake --overlays), which ones really make pixels. See The overlay report.

Any mode takes --breadcrumb FILE: the call that the replay is about to make is written to the file, as one line, for each call. A line says whether the call was begun or finished, the frame, the number of the call, the thread, the function and its arguments. The file is mapped into the memory of the checker, so what is in it when the program crashes is the call that crashed it (or, finished, the last one that was made); nothing has to be flushed.

The overlay report

--overlay-report candidates.json decides, for each draw that may be a 2D overlay, whether it really makes pixels. Some of the frames of each draw (the list says which: its first, middle and last frame) are replayed with the draw and without it (the recipe skips it), and the images are compared.

  • It writes an animated GIF and a still for each frame that changes, a JSON and an index.html per run, and flags a draw that the recipe leaves alone but is deep or covers much of the picture.
  • The page lists the draws on the left, each with a dot for every frame it was looked at in. A click goes to the draw on the right, where its frames come one after the other in the order of time, so that how it behaves over the run can be seen.
OptionEffect
--overlay-report-last-frame NLeaves out the frames after the Nth (each replay goes from the start to its frame, so the later frames cost most).
--jobs NThe work is shared between processes.
--depsThe candidates of a frame are played in one run of the frame (quick path); see below.
--overlay-report-exactReplays a candidate at a time for all of them, as a check of the quick path (the images are the same).
--overlay-report-shard-entries NA process looks at no more than N candidates (40 by default; 0: no limit). A longer list is split into more parts than --jobs, and the parts run no more than --jobs at a time.

The quick path plays the frame once for each variant (as it is, each pass left out, each draw left out, the cut frames), each from the state that the frame before left. That must be allowed by the frame's dependencies: it makes or releases no resource, and what it writes it replaces before it reads it. A frame that does not allow it, or one that fails so, is replayed a candidate at a time.

The shards exist because a replay leaves memory behind and a 32-bit process runs out of address space after a few hundred.

Where the images come from

The images that the modes compare come from the executor of each API.

APISource of the image
Direct3DIts back buffer.
OpenGLThe color buffer of the context that is current, which is the whole client area of its window, read as the back buffer after the swap. The specification leaves its content undefined after the swap; on the drivers the replay is run with it is what was presented. Its rows are put from the top and its alpha is 255, whatever the window has.

A draw that the recipe leaves out is left out whole: the calls that come before the call of the draw and belong to it (the glBegin and the vertices of a block that glEnd makes a draw of) are left out with it, as a primitive is drawn when its vertices are given. The draws of a list that is called share the call of the list, so they are left out together or not at all.

The swap of the buffers of a window is made after the executions of a frame, as the present of Direct3D is, so that the image of a frame is read before it.

The checker's source has a file for each of these ways of working (replay_runner, comparison, screenshot_mode, profile_mode, state_census_mode, overlay_report_mode), a command_line that has tests, and a main.cpp that loads the recipe and chooses.
