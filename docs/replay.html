Replay

Executing the calls of a workload again, without the program. Here it is done for both eyes: it is what the recipes are tried on, and what checks that a frame can be drawn twice.

The engine is the replay engine of the interceptor (ReplayEngineCore, a static library), see its documentation: it walks the calls of a workload one after the other, with an executor for each API, and has nothing to do with stereo. What this project adds goes in through two interfaces of the engine: a IFrameExecutor, which plays the calls of a frame (the engine presents the frame and pauses between two), and the executors of the calls, which a class can derive from. The interceptor's own player uses the plain ones.

The replay engine

StereoReplayEngineDLL is the engine of the interceptor with the frames played for the eyes of a recipe (StereoFrameExecutor), at the pace of the dumped process (PacedFrameExecutor, which wraps the other and waits for the frame, so that pacing is not part of the engine), and the Direct3D 7 calls executed for two eyes (StereoD3D7CallExecutor, which derives from the executor of the interceptor and keeps a copy of some objects for each eye). Observers hear about frames and calls.

Each API has an executor of its own, registered by its API group in the ExecutorRegistry: today DirectDraw 7 and Direct3D 7, Direct3D 8, OpenGL and the Win32 windowing calls (GDI32, User32, WGL), and another API is added with its own. The executor of DirectDraw 7 and Direct3D 7 replays a program in a window, whatever it did: the primary surface shows the window, the back buffer of a flipping chain is a surface of the replay, and a flip is a blit of it to the window. It replays the methods that the programs of the captures use, and fails on one it has no code for, rather than draw something wrong. There is no translation of one API to another. The engine remaps the objects of the dump onto the live ones, restores the arguments a call needs, replays locks and client arrays, and paces the frames.

Two eyes

With a recipe, the engine executes a frame twice: the recipe tells, for each draw, whether it is drawn once, for each eye, or not at all (RecipeApplier), and the matrix of the eye is bound to the draws that are drawn per eye (EyeMatrixBinder). A frame is rewritten (FrameRewriter) before it is run, and the images of both eyes are handed to a sink (IFrameImageSink), which is how the player shows them.

An application whose vertices no matrix moves (Kasparov draws only pre-transformed vertices: positions in pixels and the reciprocal of the depth) has no matrix to give the eyes: its recipe has a screen_parallax section, and the engine moves the x of each vertex of an affected Direct3D 7 draw before it is played (D3D7DrawRewriter, which uses Stereo::D3D7::ScreenParallax for the vertices themselves): for the left eye to the right, for the right eye to the left, by the shift of a camera moved to the side (the angle of view and the distance at which the eyes converge are the recipe's, the separation is the player's). What is at the convergence distance, such as an interface drawn flat at one depth, is not moved. A draw into a texture is moved like any other, so that the texture is the picture of the eye, and a quad that draws the texture to the screen, which is flat, is left where it is.

Two things make a frame played twice the same as a frame played once, for what the frame does not change between the eyes. First, the second execution begins where the first did: a frame may rely on what the frame before it left on the device (the Kasparov demo clears and draws without setting a render target, because the last call of the frame before it set it, and ends each frame by setting the other of the two targets that it renders to in turn), and the first execution changes that. The executor puts the state back before the second execution (save_state and restore_state: a state block for Direct3D 8; for Direct3D 7, which has none, what the replayed calls set is kept). Second, a texture which is rendered to and which a frame uses what an earlier frame left in (per_eye_textures of the recipe) has a copy for each eye: the executor makes it twice when the application makes it, sets the copy up with the same calls, and plays the calls of each eye on its own copy, so that the picture which an eye's frame renders is what that eye's next frame reads.

The programs

ProgramUse
StereoReplayPlayerA control window (ImGui, through UIHost) around the engine: choose a workload and optionally a recipe, play and pause, and see the images of both eyes in a preview. A 64-bit player starts the 32-bit one for a 32-bit workload. The window has a seek bar: a slider, a step to the next frame and a box to type a frame in (g <frame> with --console). A frame ahead is reached by hurrying through the frames before it, unpaced, and the replay pauses after it (play_to_frame of the playback controller). There is no way to jump into the middle of a workload, since the state of the device is made by every call before it: a frame back in time starts the replay over and hurries to it, and the window asks first (with a tick to stop asking). The frames are counted as the converter counts them live (the window of a running converter says Frame N), so a frame seen there can be typed here.
StereoReplayConsistencyCheckerNo window. Replays a workload several ways, hashes the images of each frame and compares the ways: that a replay is repeatable, and that a frame executed twice draws the same as once. It can write the images of a frame that differs. --ways picks the ways to replay (each is a replay of the workload from its start, so fewer are less to wait for), and --parallel replays them at the same time, each with an engine of its own: on a 3,000 frame capture of Direct3D 8 that takes 17 s instead of 47 s, and it needs the memory of all of them. --screenshot N --out file.png replays once, up to frame N, and writes that frame as a PNG: with a recipe both eyes side by side (or --eye left, right, anaglyph), without one the frame as it is. --profile replays the frames once (for the eyes of the recipe, if there is one) and says where the time of the calls went: the functions whose calls took longest in all (calls, average and slowest call), and the frames that took longest with their slowest call; the waiting for the pace of the capture is not in it. --same-frames-as other.workload replays the frames of both as they are and says whether the images are the same: it is how a slice (APIDumpSlicer) is checked against its workload. --contact-sheet K --out sheet.png writes one PNG of K frames spread evenly from --from to --to (or the end of the workload), each as a screenshot is, and says which frames are where. A frame written for a difference comes with an image of the pixels that differ (red), and how many, where and by how much.
