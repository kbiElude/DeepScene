Replay

Executing the calls of a workload again, without the program. Here it is done for both eyes: it is where recipes are tried, where a frame is checked to be drawable twice, and the proving ground for what the live converter does inside a running program.

The engine

The replay engine belongs to API Interceptor (ReplayEngineCore, a static library; see its documentation). It walks the calls of a workload one after the other with an executor for each API, and knows nothing of stereo. What this project adds goes in through two interfaces of the engine: an IFrameExecutor, which plays the calls of a frame (the engine presents the frame and pauses between two), and the executors of the calls, which a class can derive from. The result is StereoReplayEngineDLL. A program that wants to hear of frames and calls derives from NullReplayObserver, an observer that does nothing, and overrides what it cares about (the player's FrameRangeObserver, the checker's StopObserver).

Two eyes

With a recipe, the engine executes every frame twice. The recipe tells, for each draw, whether it is drawn once, for each eye, or not at all (RecipeApplier), and the matrices of the eye are bound to the draws that are drawn per eye (EyeMatrixBinder), by the rules of Stereo. A frame is rewritten (FrameRewriter) before it is run, and the two images go to a sink, which is how the player shows them.

OpenGL. The GL executor (GlCallExecutor) says when each call is made in a frame that is executed twice: what makes an object (glGen*, glCreate*) in the first execution only, since a second one would make others and the names that the replay maps would be lost; what deletes one (glDelete*) and the swap of the buffers after the last; everything else (state, matrices, draws, uploads, the lists) in each. The state is put back between the executions by GlStateSaver: the attribute stacks (glPushAttrib and glPushClientAttrib with all the bits) and the matrices, which they do not hold (the model-view and the projection matrix, and the texture matrix of each unit, as numbers). What the objects hold is not put back. Frames of Gerbera that are executed twice, with and without that, come out as they do executed once (frames 6190, 7060, 10000, 15000, 22000 and 26900 of the checker, whose second execution makes the same calls as the first).

OpenGL, the eyes. A recipe with a view_anchor_shift (see Recipes) gives the eyes parallax without a rule for a matrix: GL has none to rewrite, for the camera is built by calls on the model-view matrix. The front end says, for each draw, the call that begins its camera: the last load of that matrix that its stack descends from (a push copies it with the matrix, a pop gives back the one of the matrix below, and what is multiplied in leaves it). The call that is such an anchor for a draw that the recipe affects is played with the shift of the eye in place of what it loads: a glLoadIdentity loads the shift, a glLoadMatrixf or glLoadMatrixd loads the shift times the matrix of the program. Everything the program does next is on top, and the calls that are made relative to the camera (the position of a light, a clip plane, the planes of the coordinates of a texture) are made again for each eye, since the frame is. A call that is the anchor of draws of which some are affected and some are not, and a draw that is affected and has no anchor, stop the replay with a message that says which: the recipe leaves the draws alone, or says "draws_without_anchor": "leave". The whole of Gerbera, 27,020 frames executed twice with the recipe in apps/recipes/gerbera.json, plays without a refusal.

OpenGL, the textures. A texture that rendering fills and that a frame reads what an earlier frame left in has a copy for each eye (per_eye_textures): StereoGlCallExecutor makes the copy when the texture is first changed after the analysis knows what it is (a texture has no resource before it is given a size), with the parameters of the texture, and from then on the name that the program has is mapped to the copy of the eye, and what each unit has bound is that copy. A call that changes the texture is made for the other copy too in a frame that is executed once; in one that is executed twice each execution makes it for its own eye. The copies are deleted together. A texture that is not carried from frame to frame is shared and needs none of this: Gerbera is of that kind, and with its render target made a texture for each eye anyway the images come out as they do with one.

  • The state put back. save_state and restore_state are a state block in Direct3D 8; Direct3D 7 has none, so what the replayed calls set is kept.
  • Per-eye textures. A texture that is rendered to, and that a frame uses what an earlier frame left in (per_eye_textures of the recipe), has a copy for each eye: the executor makes it twice when the application makes it, sets the copy up with the same calls, and plays the calls of each eye on its own copy, so what an eye's frame renders is what that eye's next frame reads.
  • Vertices in pixels. A program whose vertices no matrix moves (Kasparov) has no matrix to give the eyes; the engine moves the x of each vertex of an affected Direct3D 7 draw before it is played (D3D7DrawRewriter, using Stereo::D3D7::ScreenParallax), by the screen-space formula. A draw into a texture is moved like any other, so the texture is the picture of the eye; the quad that draws the texture to the screen is flat and stays.

Seeking

There is no way to jump into the middle of a workload: the state of the device is made by every call before it. A frame back in time starts the replay over; a frame ahead is reached by hurrying through the frames before it, unpaced, not presented and not read back. A seek is then as long as the replay up to the frame, which for a long capture is long. The dependencies make most of it unnecessary.

  • What it needs: the .deps file of the workload (APIDumpDependencyExtractor; the player takes the file of the workload's name, or --deps), and for the chunk skipping the .index of the workload (APIDumpPrinter --index); without the index every chunk is read.
  • Who does it: FrameSlicePlanner (in Analysis) makes the plan, SliceSkipper carries it out, and FrameHurry runs the frames before the target unpaced.
  • Correctness is checked rather than assumed: the image of the target frame of a seek with the dependencies must be byte for byte that of the replay of every frame. The checker's screenshots, with --deps and without, are how to check it.
  • A capture where every frame reads what the previous one wrote (fr08) gains nothing: no frame is dead.
  • OpenGL: a dead frame leaves out glBegin to glEnd with the vertices between them, glClear, the array draws, glReadPixels, glCopyTexImage2D and glCopyTexSubImage2D, glDrawPixels, glBitmap and glCallList; the state, the textures and the lists are made. The calls of a list that is being made (glNewList to glEndList) are always made, so that the list has them. A call of a list is left out only if the list does draws and state and nothing of the eye's own (GlDisplayListTracker follows the lists as the calls come, and classes them as the analysis does, GlListEyeDependence); a list that loads or moves a matrix, sets a projection, a viewport or a scissor box, calls such a list, or is not defined ends the process with a message (AI_ASSERT_ALWAYS), since leaving it out would leave the matrices of the frames after it not as the capture had them. The reader does not make the content calls of a dead frame into calls at all (SliceSkipper::get_call_kept_func_factory; a segment is read by one thread, which follows glNewList and glEndList in it, so the calls of a list that is being made are kept; a list that is made across two segments ends the process the same way), and the chunks that only those calls refer to are left out; the calls of a list are left to the skipper, which knows the list. Gerbera: the images of frames 5000, 10000 and 20000 are byte for byte those of the replay of every frame, and the time falls to 3.4 s, 8.7 s and 16.4 s (3.5 s, 15.1 s and 30.7 s as it is).
  • Readbacks: the replay of a GL workload does not make glGetFloatv, nor glReadPixels when no pixel pack buffer is bound: they only write into the memory of the application, what the app computed of them is in the calls that follow as it was dumped, and a replay has no use for the values (so a second eye needs them as little). Gerbera makes 96,292 of the first and 265,968 of the second; the replay to frame 27000 takes 32.8 s (19.5 s with the dependencies), and the images are the same.
  • Texture parameters: a glTexParameteri or glTexParameterf that gives a parameter of a texture the value it has is not made (GlTextureParameterCache, one of the interceptors of the engine): a driver takes such a call as long as one that changes something, and a workload sets the same few parameters again and again. Gerbera makes 465,000 of them in a seek to frame 6954, and two thirds give a parameter the value it has: with those left out the seek takes 27.3 s instead of 29.3 s (11.2 s instead of 13.2 s to frame 6185), and the images of both eyes at frames 246, 3490, 6185, 6954, 12000 and 20000 are byte for byte those of the replay without the cache. Only the ten parameters that the twin of a texture for the other eye takes are looked at (the filters, how the coordinates wrap, the levels and their generation), so a call that is left out is one that the twin would not have changed either. The first call that gives a parameter its value is always made, since what the texture has until then is not known; and what the cache knows is forgotten, not guessed, when a call may change it without being told: a display list that sets the texture state (or is not known) is called, textures are deleted, glPopAttrib, a context is made or deleted, a call that another interceptor left out, and a replay that starts over.
  • Textures filled a part at a time: a texture whose writes all say where they landed (written_extent) is whole again once the frames that wrote it have written all of the part that any write reaches, and the versions before those do not show in it: the way back of a frame that uses it ends there (FrameSlicePlanner, compute_windows; at most 64 versions are looked at together), at the last version that replaced all of it, which holds what the writes do not reach. A frame that is made for a version it wrote is made in part, which is to say that the replay makes all of its calls but only what flows into that version (input_resource_vec) is looked for in the frames before it: the draws that sample the texture after the copy to it use the texture, and what they render is not read by any frame that is made. Gerbera has one such texture, of 1024 x 512, that 845 frames (6186 to 7060) copy the same 147 x 414 rectangle to: a seek to frame 6954 makes 3 of the frames before it instead of 739, and takes 13.1 s instead of 27.3 s (the 11 s before the cut are the state calls of the frames that are not made, and are the same at any frame of the cut). The images of both eyes at 16 frames of all the cuts are byte for byte those of the replay of every frame.
  • State that a pop undoes: in a dead frame, between a glPushAttrib and its glPopAttrib, the reader also leaves out the calls that only give a piece of state a value which the groups of the mask save (GlAttributeCoverage: capabilities, materials, lights, the texture environment and generation, blend, depth, polygon and line state, the current color, normal and texture coordinates, and the like), since nothing that is made looks at the state before the pop does away with it. What is made are the push and the pop, the calls that choose where the next ones go (the active texture unit, the matrix mode, the bound texture), the parameters of textures (they change an object, which the pop does not undo), and the state of the client (the arrays and their pointers, which glPushClientAttrib saves and glPushAttrib does not). A frame that is left out has to pop what it pushes: one that ends first ends the process with a message (AI_ASSERT_ALWAYS), as the state that it would have left is not made; and a pair that begins in a segment and ends in the next leaves out what follows the push in the first one only. Gerbera pushes all of the attributes once in each frame, and every one of its dead frames pops them: a seek to frame 6954 makes 4.9 million calls where it made 10.4 million, and takes 9.1 s where it took 13.1 s (to frame 6185: 8.2 s where it took 11.2 s; to frame 20000: 21.2 s where it took 30.3 s), with the images of both eyes at the frames that were looked at byte for byte those of the replay without it. --state-census says how many calls of each kind a seek does not make.
  • Frames are counted as the converter counts them live (the window of a running converter says Frame N), so a frame seen there can be typed here.

The programs

ProgramWhat it is
StereoReplayPlayerA window (ImGui, through UIHost of Utils) around the engine: choose a workload and optionally a recipe, play and pause, see both eyes in a preview. It has a seek bar (a slider, a step, and a box to type a frame in; g <frame> with --console); while a seek is under way the status says seeking, and paused once the frame is reached. A frame back in time asks first. The eye separation slider changes the distance for the frames from then on; when it is let go of while paused, the frame on show is played again.
StereoReplayConsistencyCheckerNo window. Replays a workload several ways and compares the images; takes screenshots; profiles; makes the overlay report. Its modes are in the next section.

In the release (see Building) the engine and the two programs are in tools\stereo_replay\, each as _x64 and _x86; a 64-bit player starts the 32-bit one for a 32-bit workload, and the 32-bit ones replay the Direct3D 7, Direct3D 8 and OpenGL captures, which are of 32-bit programs. In the source they are in src/tools/stereo_replay, in engine_dll, player and consistency_checker.

What the checker is for

The checker is the test that the engine is fit for stereo: that a replay is repeatable, and that a frame executed twice draws the same as once. It is also where everything else about a recipe is tried without a window.

ModeSays
(none) with --ways, --parallelReplays the frames as they are, again, executed twice, with the state put back, and for the eyes of the recipe; hashes each frame's image and compares the ways. It can write the images of the first frame that differs, with an image of the pixels that differ. Each way is a replay from the start; --parallel replays them at the same time, each with an engine of its own.
--screenshot N --out f.pngOne frame as a PNG: with a recipe both eyes side by side (or --eye left, right, anaglyph), without one the frame as it is. Frames before N are played once for the left eye unless --history is given; --deps makes it quick and exact.
--contact-sheet K --out f.pngOne PNG of K frames spread evenly over a range, each as a screenshot is.
--same-frames-as other.workloadWhether two workloads give the same images (exit code 3 if not): how a slice is checked against its workload.
--profileWhere the time of the calls went: the functions and the frames that took longest.
--state-censusWhat the calls of OpenGL in the frames that a seek to frame B does not make (--deps says which) do to the state of the context: per function, how many give a piece of state the value it has, and how many are overwritten by another call before a frame that is made, a push or a pop of attributes, a call of a list or another barrier looks at it; and whether each of those frames pushes and pops all that it changes.
--overlay-report candidates.jsonOf the draws that may be 2D overlays (RecipeBake --overlays), which ones really make pixels: some of the frames of each (the list says which: its first, middle and last frame) are replayed with the draw and without it (the recipe skips it), and the images are compared. A draw that changes no pixel in a frame is looked at again to say why: the frame is cut after its last call, with the draw and without it, and if the two differ it makes pixels when it is drawn and what is drawn after it covers them (overdrawn later), and if they do not it makes none then (not rasterized: transparent, clipped, outside the picture, or hidden by the depth test). It writes an animated GIF and a still for each frame that changes, a JSON and an index.html per run, and flags a draw that the recipe leaves alone but is deep or covers much of the picture. The page lists the draws on the left, each with a dot for every frame it was looked at in, and a click goes to the draw on the right, where its frames come one after the other in the order of time, so that how it behaves over the demo can be seen. --overlay-report-last-frame N leaves out the frames after the Nth (each replay goes from the start to its frame, so the later frames cost most). The work can be shared between processes (--jobs N). With --deps, the candidates of a frame are played in one run of the frame (quick path): the frame is played once for each variant (as it is, each pass left out, each draw left out, the cut frames), each from the state that the frame before left, which the frame's dependencies must allow (it makes or releases no resource, and what it writes it replaces before it reads it); a frame that does not, or one that fails so, is replayed a candidate at a time, and --overlay-report-exact does that for all of them, as a check of the quick path (the images are the same). A replay leaves some memory behind (about 1 MB in early frames, about 5 MB in later ones), and a 32-bit process that makes about 250 of them runs out of address space; so a process looks at no more than --overlay-report-shard-entries N candidates (40 by default; 0: no limit), a longer list is split into more parts than --jobs, and the parts run no more than --jobs at a time.

The images that the modes compare come from the executor of each API: Direct3D reads its back buffer, and OpenGL the color buffer of the context that is current, which is the whole client area of its window, read as the back buffer after the swap (the specification leaves it undefined there; with the driver that was tried it is what was presented, and a frame of Gerbera replayed to the same picture as the one the dumper stored for it). Its rows are put from the top and its alpha is 255, whatever the window has.

A draw that the recipe leaves out is left out whole: the calls that come before the call of the draw and belong to it (the glBegin and the vertices of a block that glEnd makes a draw of) are left out with it, as a primitive is drawn when its vertices are given. The draws of a list that is called share the call of the list, so they are left out together or not at all. The swap of the buffers of a window is made after the executions of a frame, as the present of Direct3D is, so that the image of a frame is read before it.

Any mode takes --breadcrumb FILE: the call that the replay is about to make is written to the file, as one line (whether it was begun or finished, the frame, the number of the call, the thread, the function and its arguments), for each call. The file is mapped into the memory of the checker, so what is in it when the program crashes is the call that crashed it (or, finished, the last one that was made); nothing has to be flushed.

The checker's source has a file for each of these ways of working (replay_runner, comparison, screenshot_mode, profile_mode, state_census_mode, overlay_report_mode), a command_line that has tests, and a main.cpp that loads the recipe and chooses.
