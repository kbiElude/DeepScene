Replay

Executing the calls of a workload again, without the program. Here it is done for both eyes: it is what the recipes are tried on, and what checks that a frame can be drawn twice.

The engine is the replay engine of the interceptor (ReplayEngineCore, a static library), see its documentation: it walks the calls of a workload one after the other, with an executor for each API, and has nothing to do with stereo. What this project adds goes in through two interfaces of the engine: a IFrameExecutor, which plays the calls of a frame (the engine presents the frame and pauses between two), and the executors of the calls, which a class can derive from. The interceptor's own player uses the plain ones.

The replay engine

StereoReplayEngineDLL is the engine of the interceptor with the frames played for the eyes of a recipe (StereoFrameExecutor), at the pace of the dumped process (PacedFrameExecutor, which wraps the other and waits for the frame, so that pacing is not part of the engine), and the Direct3D 7 calls executed for two eyes (StereoD3D7CallExecutor, which derives from the executor of the interceptor and keeps a copy of some objects for each eye). Observers hear about frames and calls.

Each API has an executor of its own, registered by its API group in the ExecutorRegistry: today DirectDraw 7 and Direct3D 7, Direct3D 8, OpenGL and the Win32 windowing calls (GDI32, User32, WGL), and another API is added with its own. The executor of DirectDraw 7 and Direct3D 7 replays a program in a window, whatever it did: the primary surface shows the window, the back buffer of a flipping chain is a surface of the replay, and a flip is a blit of it to the window. It replays the methods that the programs of the captures use, and fails on one it has no code for, rather than draw something wrong. There is no translation of one API to another. The engine remaps the objects of the dump onto the live ones, restores the arguments a call needs, replays locks and client arrays, and paces the frames.

Two eyes

With a recipe, the engine executes a frame twice: the recipe tells, for each draw, whether it is drawn once, for each eye, or not at all (RecipeApplier), and the matrix of the eye is bound to the draws that are drawn per eye (EyeMatrixBinder). A frame is rewritten (FrameRewriter) before it is run, and the images of both eyes are handed to a sink (IFrameImageSink), which is how the player shows them.

An application whose vertices no matrix moves (Kasparov draws only pre-transformed vertices: positions in pixels and the reciprocal of the depth) has no matrix to give the eyes: its recipe has a screen_parallax section, and the engine moves the x of each vertex of an affected Direct3D 7 draw before it is played (D3D7DrawRewriter, which uses Stereo::D3D7::ScreenParallax for the vertices themselves): for the left eye to the right, for the right eye to the left, by the shift of a camera moved to the side (the angle of view and the distance at which the eyes converge are the recipe's, the separation is the player's). What is at the convergence distance, such as an interface drawn flat at one depth, is not moved. A draw into a texture is moved like any other, so that the texture is the picture of the eye, and a quad that draws the texture to the screen, which is flat, is left where it is. A vertex at an edge of the picture (within half a percent of the width) is not moved from it: not to the right at the left edge, nor to the left at the right one, so the ground that reaches the side of the picture still does, and no gap of the colour behind it opens there (a wedge at the bottom left of frame 3702 at a separation of 1.055). The edge of such a polygon is slanted a little by it, which is what it costs.

Two things make a frame played twice the same as a frame played once, for what the frame does not change between the eyes. First, the second execution begins where the first did: a frame may rely on what the frame before it left on the device (the Kasparov demo clears and draws without setting a render target, because the last call of the frame before it set it, and ends each frame by setting the other of the two targets that it renders to in turn), and the first execution changes that. The executor puts the state back before the second execution (save_state and restore_state: a state block for Direct3D 8; for Direct3D 7, which has none, what the replayed calls set is kept). Second, a texture which is rendered to and which a frame uses what an earlier frame left in (per_eye_textures of the recipe) has a copy for each eye: the executor makes it twice when the application makes it, sets the copy up with the same calls, and plays the calls of each eye on its own copy, so that the picture which an eye's frame renders is what that eye's next frame reads.

The programs

ProgramUse
StereoReplayPlayerA control window (ImGui, through UIHost of the Utils library) around the engine: choose a workload and optionally a recipe, play and pause, and see the images of both eyes in a preview. A 64-bit player starts the 32-bit one for a 32-bit workload. The window has a seek bar: a slider, a step to the next frame and a box to type a frame in (g <frame> with --console). A frame ahead is reached by hurrying through the frames before it, unpaced, and the replay pauses after it (play_to_frame of the playback controller). There is no way to jump into the middle of a workload, since the state of the device is made by every call before it: a frame back in time starts the replay over and hurries to it, and the window asks first (with a tick to stop asking). While a seek is under way the State line says seeking (the status of the engine has is_seeking: a frame was asked for with play_to_frame and it is not the next one), and paused once the frame is reached. The frames that a seek passes through are not presented and not read back for the preview. With the dependencies between the frames of the workload (.deps, made by APIDumpDependencyExtractor; the player takes the file that has the name of the workload, or --deps) it also leaves out what the frames do not need to draw: a frame is dead if nothing that the target frame or any frame after it reads comes of it, and a dead frame is played as it is (once, for one eye) with its draws, clears, copies and uploads left out, and only the calls that set state or make objects made (FrameSlicePlanner, SliceSkipper). A frame which writes only part of a resource keeps the version before it needed; a frame which writes all of one ends the way back. The frames after the target are all made, so the replay can go on from it. The reader of the workload leaves those frames out too: the data chunks (vertices, textures) that only the content calls of dead frames refer to are not read, which is most of what a seek spends its time on (the replay thread waits for the segments to be decoded, and a chunk is allocated, cleared and filled for nothing). It needs the index of the workload (the file name of the workload with .index added, beside it, made by APIDumpPrinter --index); without it every chunk is read. The filter is set when a seek is asked for and applies to the segments that are yet to be read, and a seek that is asked for before the last one has been reached starts the replay over, since the segments read ahead would lack chunks that the new target needs. A seek of the consistency checker to frame 20,000 of Kasparov went from 14.6 s to 6.5 s, and to 4.9 s with 6 threads reading ahead in place of 3 (the segments are small without those chunks, so the threads fit in the memory; the decompression, 51 GB of content before that frame, is the most of what is left, and it speeds up with the threads), and its seeks to frames 1,500, 9,850 and 15,000 with a recipe (both eyes, separation 2.0) give files that are byte for byte those of the replay of every frame. Measured on the 39,000-frame capture of Kasparov, with a recipe (both eyes), a seek to frame 20,000 takes 18 s with the dependencies (98 s without), and to the end 30 s; the images of the target frame are byte for byte those of the replay of every frame (checked at frames 3,000, 12,000, 20,000 and 32,764, both eyes). The eye separation slider changes the distance for the frames played from then on; when it is let go of while the replay is paused, the frame on show is played again (a seek to it, so it takes as long as one), unless the box under it is unticked. fr08 gains nothing: what its frames write is read by the next one, so none is dead. Without the dependencies the hurry goes at some 700 frames a second with no recipe: frame 5,000 is reached in about 8 s, frame 20,000 in about 28 s, and the end in about a minute. The frames are counted as the converter counts them live (the window of a running converter says Frame N), so a frame seen there can be typed here.
StereoReplayConsistencyCheckerNo window. Replays a workload several ways, hashes the images of each frame and compares the ways: that a replay is repeatable, and that a frame executed twice draws the same as once. It can write the images of a frame that differs. --ways picks the ways to replay (each is a replay of the workload from its start, so fewer are less to wait for), and --parallel replays them at the same time, each with an engine of its own: on a 3,000 frame capture of Direct3D 8 that takes 17 s instead of 47 s, and it needs the memory of all of them. --screenshot N --out file.png replays once, up to frame N, and writes that frame as a PNG: with a recipe both eyes side by side (or --eye left, right, anaglyph), without one the frame as it is. With a recipe, the frames before N are played once, for the left eye alone, unless --history is given: then they are played for both eyes as a player does, so that what the eyes carry over from frame to frame is right (and the replay is slow). --deps file.deps (see APIDumpDependencyExtractor) makes that quick and exact: the frames which nothing after N reads are played without drawing (see the seek of the player). Taking the same screenshot with --history and with --deps and comparing the two files is the check that the slice leaves out nothing which matters. --profile replays the frames once (for the eyes of the recipe, if there is one) and says where the time of the calls went: the functions whose calls took longest in all (calls, average and slowest call), and the frames that took longest with their slowest call; the waiting for the pace of the capture is not in it. --same-frames-as other.workload replays the frames of both as they are and says whether the images are the same: it is how a slice (APIDumpSlicer) is checked against its workload. --contact-sheet K --out sheet.png writes one PNG of K frames spread evenly from --from to --to (or the end of the workload), each as a screenshot is, and says which frames are where. A frame written for a difference comes with an image of the pixels that differ (red), and how many, where and by how much. --overlay-probe candidates.json --recipe file.json --dump-dir dir takes the list that RecipeBake --overlays writes (the draws that may be 2D overlays) and finds which of them are drawn in the picture: the frame where each is first seen is replayed for the left eye with the draw and without it (the recipe skips it), and the images are compared. It writes for each candidate that makes pixels an animated GIF that alternates (every 0.7 s) between the frame as it is and the same frame with the pixels the draw makes in magenta, so that what the magenta covers can be seen by looking at the picture flip, and a still PNG (the frame dimmed, the pixels in magenta), candidates.json with the number of those pixels, and index.html to look at them, with a filter and a button that copies the key of a draw. For each draw it says how many pixels it makes and what part of the picture that is, and how deep the vertices of its pass are (from the list); a draw that the recipe leaves alone and whose pass is deep (deep_depth), or that makes more than two fifths of the picture (covers_much_of_picture), gets a red badge and an outline, and the page can show those alone: an overlay is a flat part of the picture, so the image of such a draw is the thing to look at. On the old Kasparov recipe, which left a whole 3D scene alone, it flags all six draws of that pass (one makes 63% of the picture, the sky and the ground behind the towers). RecipeBake --check says the same without replaying anything: it exits with 6 for a pass that the recipe leaves alone and whose vertices are at many depths, or that a replay leaves alone and the converter that plays the application as it runs does not (it cannot tell passes apart, so a recipe should name draws). A draw is left out in its own pass only (a recipe name for it: pass_draws, a pass key with a draw key), since a draw structure is often in several passes, and a scene that is drawn by another pass of the same structure would be left out with it: that once made a ceiling look like an overlay of the whole picture. A pass that makes no difference left out whole is left out once for all its draws. The work can be shared between processes of the program (--jobs N, 1 by default: none), by what the replays cost (the draws of a pass in a frame together). Each replay decodes the workload from its start, and that is what takes the time. It is bound by the bandwidth of the memory, and since the reader uses 6 threads when it leaves chunks out, one process saturates a machine of 8 cores: the same seek of Kasparov to frame 34,572 took 9.0 s alone and 17.3 s each for two at once, 40 s each for four and 80 s each for eight, which is no more work in the time. (With 3 threads, as it was, two processes did 1.5 times the work of one; the threads do that now.) The 62 candidates of Kasparov took 13 minutes in one process with 3 threads, and 8.6 minutes in four. --deps makes each replay quick; a seek of the checker to frame 20,000 takes 4.9 s (6.5 s with 3 reader threads, 14.6 s before the chunks of the dead frames were left out).

In the release (see Building) the engine and the two programs are in tools\stereo_replay\, each as _x64 and as _x86 (StereoReplayPlayer_x86.exe, StereoReplayConsistencyChecker_x86.exe, StereoReplayEngineDLL_x86.dll): the 32-bit ones replay the Direct3D 7 and Direct3D 8 captures, which are of 32-bit programs. In the source they are in src/tools/stereo_replay, in folders engine_dll, player and consistency_checker; the checker has a file for each of its ways of working (see Components).
