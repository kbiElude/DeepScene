Recipes

A recipe is the one file that says how to turn one program into stereo: which draws are the scene, which are flat, and the numbers that go with them. Everything that needs to know about a program reads its recipe; nothing at play time reads the capture.

What a recipe holds

A recipe is JSON, in two kinds of parts. A person (often with an LLM reading the analysis) writes the decisions; RecipeBake adds the measurements in a section called baked and never touches the rest.

The numbers in the right-hand column are what Stereo explains: the convergence distance, the ceiling of the separation, and the pushing of the interface. Two version numbers guard the file: key_scheme_version must equal that of the build that reads it (or the keys would not match anything), and the baked signature_scheme_version the same for signatures. The loader refuses a recipe of another scheme instead of guessing.

Baking: from what is known offline to what is known live

An offline replay knows more than a converter inside a running program. The replay knows the pass a draw belongs to; the converter, when the draw is issued, does not, since the pass is still open. A converter can only match a draw by its signature and the view matrix. The baker bridges the two.

RecipeBake has four modes. Only the first writes anything.

CommandWhat it does
RecipeBake recipe.json file.passes [--deps file.deps] [-o baked.json]Bakes: writes the baked section into the recipe (or into the file -o names) and prints what it found. Without --deps a texture that frames use by turns is missed, so per_eye_textures comes from the totals of the analysis only.
RecipeBake --check recipe passesChecks a recipe against a capture, and writes nothing. Lists the frame shapes and draw signatures that the recipe does not know (exit 5), and a pass left alone whose vertices are at many depths, which is a 3D scene seen from one place and not an overlay, or one that a replay leaves alone and the converter inside the program does not (exit 6). It also lists the kinds of draws (signature and size) whose draws are not all of one kind by what the analysis saw them do (some in the space of the picture and others not, some over a 3D scene and others not, some in the space of the eye and others not). The automatic layers leave those out; name them in the recipe if each is one thing. That part is informational and changes no exit code.
RecipeBake --overlays recipe passesPrints the draws that may be 2D overlays, by the recipe and by the hint of the analysis, and where the two disagree.
RecipeBake --scenes recipe passes [--screen-degrees N]Prints, for each scene (kind of frame): where its frames are in the run (the cuts), how deep it is, where the eyes converge, how far apart they may be, and the parallax of its nearest and farthest surfaces as a part of the picture width and as an angle on a screen of N degrees (default 90).

The exit codes are 0 (done), 1 (bad arguments), 2 (a file cannot be read), 3 (the recipe cannot be baked), 4 (the file cannot be written), and 5 and 6 from --check.

Tuning comfort with --scenes

Pass the width of the headset screen the limits were tuned for, as in --screen-degrees 73.6 for a screen 73.6 degrees wide: the limits are then the widths that make 1.5 and 1 degrees. The limits of a recipe are parts of the width of the picture, so a recipe made for one width is weaker or stronger on another. Keep the near limit at about 1.5 degrees and the far limit at about 1, check the scenes that are flattest, and set what a cut needs in scene_overrides. The converter can write frames of each scene to look at (APISTEREO_DUMP_SCENES; see Live conversion).

How one is made

The steps are captured in Adding an app: capture, extract, investigate the analysis (an LLM reading the query tool's JSON is good at this), draft the rules, bake, check offline with the replay tools, try live. The recipes that ship are in apps/recipes, one per supported program; between them they cover these kinds:

Kind of programWhat its recipe uses
Direct3D 8, matriceseye_offset and eye_convergence rules on SetTransform, comfort, an unaffected list.
Direct3D 7, vertices in pixelsno rules; comfort, screen_parallax for its pre-transformed vertices.
OpenGLno rules; view_anchor_shift, cache_pixel_reads, pushed (with part_cover_keys), unaffected, comfort with measure_depth, balance_convergence, camera_attached_depth, eye_space_cover_keys, part_parallax and scene_overrides.

The format

RecipeLoader checks everything that needs no program: names, numbers and keys are valid (keys are the strings APIDumpAnalysisQuery prints), and no unknown field is left. The keys and the baked section do not depend on the API. The rules section does: a rule names the call it applies to (and the state of that call) in the terms of its API.

Three fields cannot be left out: recipe_version (0), key_scheme_version and rules. A section that a recipe does not need is left out; the sections below say what that means.

A small recipe

This is the whole hand-written part of a recipe for a Direct3D 7 program whose vertices are in pixels (the baked section is added after it):

{
  "recipe_version": 0,
  "key_scheme_version": 1,
  "target": {
    "name": "Example",
    "description": "A Direct3D 7 demo"
  },
  "comfort": {
    "max_near_parallax": 0.03,
    "overlay_parallax": 0.05
  },
  "screen_parallax": {
    "convergence_distance": 5,
    "field_of_view_degrees": 90
  },
  "rules": []
}

A recipe for a Direct3D 8 program has rules that change matrices instead, and an unaffected list of the passes that are the interface. A recipe for OpenGL has "rules": [], which must still be there, and gets its eyes from view_anchor_shift. The sections follow, in the order a recipe is usually written.

The top of the file

FieldMeaning
recipe_version0. Required.
key_scheme_versionMust equal that of the build that reads the file, or the keys would not match. Required.
targetOptional. name and description, text for people.
known_shape_keysOptional. The frame shapes the recipe was made for. Left out: no frame is unknown.
on_unknown_frameWhat to do with a frame that is not in known_shape_keys: mono (default), skip or fail.
cache_pixel_readsOptional, off by default. OpenGL: the reads of one pixel that the program makes in a row (a grid of them, a thousand in a frame, each a wait for the graphics card) are given from a picture of the left eye that is read once.
rulesThe rules for matrices (below). Required; may be empty.

Naming draws

Four sections name draws: unaffected, affected, skipped and pushed. They share a vocabulary of keys (the strings the analysis tools print), and a draw can be in more than one section: a draw can be both affected and pushed.

SectionWhat it says about the draws it names
unaffectedThe rules leave them alone: a 2D overlay. All other draws are affected.
affectedAffected, inside an unaffected pass. It only has draw_keys.
skippedNot played, in either eye. The calls that set their state are.
pushedMoved toward the viewer by comfort.overlay_parallax, whatever else they are: the draws that have content of their own (text, logos, sprites), as against a fade, a wipe or a filter that works on the picture below it, which stay as they are.

The lists a section can have:

ListNamesIn
pass_keysEvery draw of these passes.unaffected, skipped, pushed
draw_keysThese draws, wherever they are.all four
pass_drawsThis draw in this pass only: { "pass_key": "0x...", "draw_key": "0x..." }. A draw structure is often in several passes (a scene, and the pass that draws over it), and a converter that plays the application as it runs does not know the passes, so it cannot tell this from the draw.unaffected, skipped, pushed
cover_keysDraws of this key and size (the cover key of a draw: its structure and its vertex count): the parts of an object that are drawn alike, of which only some are to be moved. They are drawn once for both eyes and pushed by the whole of comfort.overlay_parallax. The replay keeps the rules for such a draw whose camera it shares with a draw that the rules affect; the converter in the application does not.pushed
part_cover_keysLike cover_keys, for the parts of an object that protrude from it (the eyes of a robot): pushed by comfort.part_parallax, moved by the depth of their origin (as the eyes would move it), and held to the nearest and the farthest parallax of the scene.pushed

pass_keys and pass_draws are for the replay, which knows the pass of a draw. A converter that plays the application as it runs does not, and leaves them out: it writes a warning to its log for each such list.

Which name wins

When a draw is named in unaffected and in affected at different levels, the most specific name wins.

Comfort

The comfort section says how much the eyes can be asked to do. Without it, no scene has a limit. All parallax numbers are parts of the width of the picture. Stereo explains how they are used.

Limits

FieldMeaning
max_near_parallaxHow far apart the pictures of the eyes may be at the nearest surface of a scene, as a part of the width of the picture (above 0, up to 1).
max_far_parallaxThe same at the farthest surface (above 0, up to 1). With a screen two metres away, the pictures of what is at infinity may be no further apart than the eyes of a person (65 mm is 0.014 of a width of 4.7 m); the narrower the angle of view, the sooner it matters.
near_fractionWhich part of the surfaces of a scene (by vertices) is its nearest. Above 0, below 1; default 0.001, the nearest thousandth.
balance_convergenceWith both limits, the eyes converge where the whole depth of a scene fits them best (the nearest surface has all of the near limit and the farthest all of the far one), and not at the median depth of its vertices.
scene_overridesWhat is said of single scenes (cuts), by the shape key of their frames: { "shape": "0x...", "max_separation": 4.0 } with any of convergence_distance, max_far_parallax, max_near_parallax and max_separation. The last two win over the limits of the recipe; the distance wins over the balance.
measure_depthOpenGL. The converter reads the depth buffer before each pass is cleared and takes the nearest and farthest surfaces of the frame from it (a frame later: the read is not waited for), in place of what was baked for the scene. The baked values cannot see display lists, nor a camera that flies past a near surface.

Pushing the interface

FieldMeaning
overlay_parallaxHow far toward the viewer the draws that are left alone (unaffected) can be pushed, if they are made to what is presented: the two pictures of them are that far apart, as a part of the width of the picture (0 to 0.5). This is what the application wants: VR Player pushes by it, with no control to turn it off. 0 or left out: the application wants none. Draws of pre-transformed vertices are pushed the same way (every x moved by half the amount, in pixels, the other way for each eye), unless they cover the whole view.
part_parallaxOptional. The same for the parts of an object that protrude from it and are named in pushed.part_cover_keys (the eyes of a robot): they are nearer than the rest of the object by little, so less. 0 or left out: as much as overlay_parallax.

Draws that are attached to the camera

FieldMeaning
camera_attached_depthAbove 0, below 1. A draw whose origin is nearer the camera than this part of the distance of convergence (an interface or a sky drawn with a perspective projection) is made as it is for both eyes.
eye_space_cover_keysDraws (by signature and size, as in pushed) that the application makes with a model-view of the identity because it has put their vertices in the space of the eye (the knobs of a control panel). They are not taken for attached to the camera whatever their origin says, and are given the parallax of the depth of their vertices.
automatic_layersThe recipe also uses the layers that the baker finds (flat_layer_draws, overlay_draws and eye_space_draws, under baked). Off by default: the recipe plays as it names its draws.

Vertices already in pixels

A program whose vertices are pre-transformed (Direct3D's x and y in pixels, and the reciprocal of the depth) has no matrix to change. The screen_parallax section is for it; without it, such vertices are left as they are.

FieldMeaning
convergence_distanceThe distance at which the eyes converge, in the units of the camera of the application; what is at it is not moved. It is used for a scene that the analysis found nothing in. The others converge at the distance that RecipeBake baked (scene_convergence), and are no further apart than its limit (scene_max_separation: from comfort and the nearest surfaces). Above 0; it must be said.
field_of_view_degreesThe angle of view of the width of the picture (above 0, below 180; default 90). There is no projection to read it from.

The camera of an OpenGL program

An OpenGL program does not set its camera with a call of one matrix: it builds it with calls on the model-view matrix (loads, multiplies, pushes and pops). The call that loads the matrix a draw comes from (a glLoadIdentity or a glLoadMatrix) loads the shift of the eye on top of what it loads, so everything the program does next is on top of it. The view_anchor_shift section turns that on.

x + h + (h / d) * z in the space of the camera, with h half of the eye separation and d the convergence distance: a camera moved to the side, with what is at d not apart.
FieldMeaning
convergence_distanceWhere the eyes converge for a scene that has no distance of its own (scene_convergence), in the units of the camera of the application. Left out: the eyes are moved apart and do not converge.
draws_without_anchorWhat to do with a draw that the recipe affects and whose camera no call begins (an interface drawn with an identity projection). "fail" (default) stops the replay and says the keys of the draws, so that the recipe can leave them alone; "leave" plays them as they are.

Rules for matrices

A rule changes a matrix that the program sets. It names the call in the terms of its API: IDirect3DDevice8::SetTransform or IDirect3DDevice7::SetTransform (the table is in recipe_loader_rules.cpp). The example is Direct3D 8; a recipe for another API names that API's calls.

"rules": [
  {
    "name": "scene camera",
    "call": "IDirect3DDevice8::SetTransform",
    "when": { "state": "D3DTS_VIEW", "matrix": "perspective" },
    "action": { "type": "eye_offset", "order": "post", "axis": "x" }
  }
]

When a rule applies

FieldMeaning
nameOptional.
callThe function the rule is about.
when.stateA D3DTS_* name (D3DTRANSFORMSTATE_* for Direct3D 7), or a number.
when.matrixany (default), perspective or orthographic.
when.pass_keysThe call is in one of these pass structures.
when.shape_keysThe frame is one of these shapes.

What a rule does

action.typeWhat it does
multiplyMultiplies the matrix of the program by left or right (16 numbers, row by row) for each eye. order is post (M * X) or pre (X * M).
replacePuts left or right in place of the matrix.
eye_offsetMultiplies by a translation along axis (x, y or z) of half the eye separation. The separation is a setting of the replay (a command-line option, the depth choice), not a part of the recipe. order as for multiply.
eye_convergenceTakes nothing. For a rule about the perspective projection (D3DTS_PROJECTION), the projection of each eye is sheared so that what is at the convergence distance is at the same place in both eyes: it seems to be at the screen. The distance of a draw is the one the baked section has for its signature (convergence), unless the replay sets one for all; with neither, the rule changes nothing. The rule for the view has to be eye_offset.

The baked section

Made from the sections above by RecipeBake; do not edit it. It has no API in it.

EntryWhat it holds
signature_scheme_versionMust equal this build's, or the signatures would not match.
known_signaturesThe signatures the analysis saw. A draw of another is one the recipe knows nothing of.
draw_rulesWhat a draw is, by what is known when it is issued. Each is { "signature", "class", "pushed", "when" }: class is affected, skipped or unaffected; pushed (optional) says the draw is moved toward the viewer; when (optional) is { "constant": "view_matrix", "equals_any": [ [16 numbers] ] }, the only constant a rule can name, and applies while the constant has one of those values. Rules are tried in order, and a draw that none applies to is affected.
scene_convergence{ "shape", "distance" }: the distance at which the eyes converge in a kind of frame, by the shape key of its frames. The converter follows the frame before, and uses the distance of the draw (convergence) for a shape that is not listed.
scene_max_separation{ "shape", "separation" }: how far apart the eyes may be in a scene, in the units of the camera, baked from comfort and the nearest surfaces. A scene that is not listed has no limit.
convergence{ "signature", "distance" }: where the eyes converge for the draws of a signature, measured from how deep the passes that draw it are, in the units of the camera.
per_eye_textures{ "description_key", "ordinal" }: the textures that are rendered to and that a frame uses what an earlier frame left in (see the dependencies; RecipeBake --deps), which each eye has a copy of. Told by the key of how the texture was made and by the number of the textures made like it before it, so a replay knows when the application makes it.
covering_drawsThe keys (signature and size) of the draws that cover the whole of the view (a flash, a fade). Found by the analysis, and not pushed toward the viewer, since moving them would leave a strip bare.
side_reaching_drawsThe keys (signature and size) of the flat draws that reach from the left edge of the picture to the right one (a bar, a scroller). Found by the analysis from where their vertices were, and pushed with the picture made wider by the push, so that neither edge is bare.
flat_layer_drawsOnly with comfort.automatic_layers. The keys of the draws made in the space of the picture (vertices in pixels, or an orthographic projection) and not always over a 3D scene, or that work on a copy of the picture: left as they are.
overlay_drawsOnly with comfort.automatic_layers. The keys of the draws made in the space of the picture and, in every frame they are in, after a draw with a perspective projection: left as they are and pushed toward the viewer by overlay_parallax.
eye_space_drawsOnly with comfort.automatic_layers. The keys of the draws whose object is at the camera (the origin is at the depth of 0) and whose vertices are all in front of it, at a real depth: their vertices are in the space of the eye, so they are given the parallax of their depth and are not taken for attached to the camera. A sky around the camera has vertices behind it, and is not one.
